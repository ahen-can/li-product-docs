Term Insurance
A term insurance policy offers life cover for a chosen term, with the nominee receiving the sum assured if the insured passes away during the policy period. The lump sum one receives can be used to support essential future needs, like children's education, clothing, food, and existing liabilities. But before buying a term plan, one must know how to choose the right coverage based on their income, expenses, future goals and outstanding liabilities. Remember, a term policy is a pure protection plan and should not be treated as a savings option. If the insured survives the term period, there's no payment made. The premiums paid are only returned if the term policy has a return of premium variant or a zero cost exit option. What is Term Insurance?
A term life policy is nothing but simply a form of life insurance cover, to offer financial
protection to your family, by way of a small sum paid regularly as premium over a fixed period
i.e. the “term”. Upon death of the insured person during the policy term, the nominee shall
receive the sum assured as per the terms of the policy. If the insured person survives this term,
then there is generally no payout, unless the insured person has opted for a term life insurance
plan with a built-in ROP (Return Of Premium) feature. The Ageas Federal term life insurance plan

make certain that, in future your loved ones don’t feel the financial gap caused by your sudden

Common Features Of Term Life Insurance
Term life insurance plans are basically financial protection instruments designed for your family’s security, future financial stability and the policyholder’s peace of mind. The most common features of a term life policy are:
Pure Protection Plan
Term life insurance helps reduce financial strain on your family, understanding that you aren’t any longer around to shoulder them.
Large coverage, low cost
Term life insurance, when purchased at a young age, passes on the advantage of lower premiums to the policyholder, in order to unlock a sum assured worth even crores of rupees, upon the death of the life assured within the policy term.
Flexible policy term & premium paying term
For premium paying term and policy term, you can choose whatever is convenient to you according to your age, health, lifestyle, major commitments, income, occupation and life stage. Pick a policy term that works best for your financial responsibilities - be it short-term or long-term. And opt for the premium paying term which ideally suits your cash flow - be it regular pay, limited pay or single pay.
Tax benefits
Enjoy tax savings on premiums and payouts both. (This applies according to the prevailing tax laws in India.)
Customizable riders
Your term life policy can be further enhanced with riders such as critical illness, accidental death benefit, and / or disability protection to strengthen your overall financial security and widen your coverage
Multiple payout options
You can decide how you would like your family to receive the death benefit from your term life insurance, whether lump sum or monthly income or a mix of the two. In this way, the maturity payout can truly support them in a way that’s most helpful for them, be it in the form of regular payments or a one-time benefit.
Early entry age, longer protection period
You can start as early as 18 years of age. Since you are allowed to start sooner, term life policy offers protection all along your prime earning years securing your family during the entire policy term. The policy duration ideally stretches up until your key financial liabilities are completed or till you reach retirement age. So, you can enjoy peace of mind for a longer duration if you start early, because your family remains protected under the policy i.e. till they are financially dependent on you.
Alterable policy
An excellent feature of the term life policy is that it adapts with your life stages. You can increase your term life insurance with a top-up plan, add riders to it, and buy another term life policy to increase the total sum assured. You can also choose your payout method to suit your financial needs, so that when life’s most cherished milestones (marriage, parenthood, dream home buying, and retirement) arrive, you are ready for it financially, and your policy stays relevant throughout to support your loved ones even in your absence.
Non-linked plan
A term insurance is not a financial instrument that’s dependent on market ups and down. Since it is a non-linked plan, you need not worry about market volatility impacting your financial decision. The returns indeed from this smart decision are - pure protection for your dependents and peace of mind for you. The sum assured is determined at the time of purchase, already known to you, with no shocks later (provided, honest declarations have been made in the application). Market risks have no other role to play in your term life policy. The concept of term insurance is extremely simple: Coverage amount stays stable, and is guaranteed to the nominees of the policy, becoming payable upon the event of the insured person’s untimely death, within the policy term as decided

What Are The Payout Options Available In Term Life Insurance?
Your term insurance policy recognizes that a one-size-fits-all approach may not be ideal and beneficial, especially during claim payouts. Hence, irrespective of when the death benefit shall become payable to the nominees of the policy, the term life insurance cover offers this option - to determine the payout mode - directly to the policyholder, right at the start before they buy the term plan. The policyholder can thus choose a method that aligns with their family’s financial responsibilities. Which means you get to pick one of the following payout options that’s most convenient and suited for your family’s evolving needs.
Here’s how customizing payouts works in your favour:
Lump sum payout
Under this option, the full sum assured is paid upfront, in one go. This works best for families that need a lump sum amount for their immediate liabilities such as loan repayment, bigger expenses like that of their child’s marriage etc.
Monthly / Regular Income payout
As the name suggests, under this, you can choose to enable smaller payments but regular income for your nominees instead of one single payment as a whole. This helps in ensuring a fixed source of earnings for the loved ones of the deceased, acting as an income replacement option for them. Unlike lump sum payout, wherein a safety corpus can be created, in regular income payout, stable financial support for a certain period of time is prioritized over wealth creation, covering the everyday expenses and short-term goals of the family members.
Combination of lump sum + income payout
This integrates the benefits of both the payout options. Here, a portion of the sum assured is paid as a lump sum amount. The remaining part is paid as regular payouts during a chosen period, thus helping achieve the family’s immediate and long-term financial goals.

Term Insurance : Important Jargons Explained
Premium
The amount that you must pay regularly to the insurer to keep your term insurance coverage on.
Sum Assured
The death benefit / coverage amount / claim payout that the family (nominee) receives upon the death of the policyholder during the policy term
Policy Term
The number of years for which your policy stays active and your family enjoys coverage / protection.
Premium paying term (PPT)
This is not the same as a policy term. PPT is the number of years you want to pay your premiums for. If you have a policy term of 40 years, and a PPT of 20 years, after the first 20 years you don’t need to pay any premiums, yet you can stay protected under the term life insurance plan for the entire duration of 40 years.
Nominee
The individual (appointed by policyholder) who is entitled to receive the sum assured (claim amount) upon the death of the policyholder during the policy term.
Grace Period
The window provided (extra time given after due date) to pay the overdue premiums in order to avoid policy lapse.
Riders
Additional covers enabled optionally alongside your base term life policy for enhanced protection and wider coverage.
Exclusions
Specific situations / causes of death which are not included in your term insurance cover (for instance, suicide in the first year of policy).
Underwriting
The process undertaken to assess your risk profile; also decide your premium and eligibility based on your age, health, coverage you have applied for, income, lifestyle and your financial commitments towards your family's future.
Claim settlement ratio (CSR)
The percentage of claims settled by the insurance company out of the total claims received in a year. The higher the claim settlement ratio, the stronger the insurer’s trustworthiness. Which means that greater are your chances of a hassle-free claim settlement experience with the insurance company. The CSR metric helps you gauge the insurance company’s commitment to honor your claim during critical times.
Return Of Premium (ROP)
A feature / policy variant that you can opt for during the purchase of the term life insurance plan, in case you wish to obtain back all the premiums paid till maturity, if you outlive the policy term.
Revival Period
The window (maximum time limit) provided to reactivate a term life policy that has lapsed due to outstanding premiums.

What Is A Term Plan?
Term insurance is the most simple and straightforward form of life insurance, which is not only affordable, but also highly preferred and considered essential by insurance experts for its coverage size. In case something happens to you during the policy duration (policy term), the term life insurance plan promises financial security to your loved ones. Since term life policies are primarily designed to act as financial instruments for family protection and not high-risk market-linked investments yielding attractive returns, there is generally no payout at maturity if you survive the term (unless you opted for a term life insurance with ‘Return Of Premium’ plan). The key highlight of any term insurance policy is its ‘low cost of premium for a high coverage amount’ benefit. So, a 25-year-old young professional, with clean health records and a non-smoker status, can buy a Rs. 1 crore term insurance plan at an annual premium of just a few thousands. If you pass away during the term, the lump sum payout from your term insurance cover protects your family members from financial distress, especially because your income and earnings are no longer available to them. Your dear ones can use this death benefit to repay your loans, fund their dreams, afford major milestones like higher education, marriage, etc., invest in other financial instruments for a better future, maintain their standard of living, live with dignity in their old age / retirement period, all this and more while keeping you in their memories for your one wise decision, your sheer commitment to secure their future with the best term policy. You can also customize your term insurance coverage for comprehensive protection by adding riders like accidental death benefit, critical illness cover, waiver of premium, and / or disability cover.

Can I Have Multiple Term Insurance Policies?
Yes, you can buy multiple term insurance policies from different insurance companies. This allows you to diversify benefits from several term insurance covers while also scaling protection with increased sum assured from multiple plans. However, make sure to disclose about each of your term life policies to all your insurers because insurance companies generally tend to check the overall eligibility of the policyholder to ensure alignment of your income with your desired total coverage amount.
Multiple term insurance covers means more flexibility for the policyholder because you need not overpay or overcommit at an early age. Instead, your term life insurance covers can be structured based on your financial responsibilities at different life stages. Your nominees can claim payouts from all policies you hold at the time of death, thus leading to lump sum benefits helping in their immediate and long-term financial relief.
Example: Anuradha, a 28-year-old non-smoker, healthy female, buys a Rs. 50 lakh term insurance plan, with the goal of providing financially for elderly care expenses of her parents, upon the likelihood of her untimely death. At 35, after marriage and taking a home loan, she buys an additional Rs. 1 crore term life insurance policy to provide coverage for her spouse and loan obligations. Later at 42, she adds another Rs. 50 lakh term insurance cover for future education expenses of her 2 children. Currently, Anuradha holds three policies: Rs. 50 lakh + Rs. 1 crore + Rs. 50 lakh = Rs. 2 crore total cover amount.
If Anuradha passes away during the policy term, nominees of Anuradha’s term insurance covers can claim death benefits from all three plans mentioned above, provided full disclosures were made at the time of their purchase.
Can My Claim Be Affected If I Pick Up Smoking Or Drinking Habits After Buying Term Insurance?
During purchase of a term life policy, your premium is usually determined after assessing your risk profile, which is calculated on the basis of your age, your current health status, medical conditions if any and lifestyle habits that are impacting your health. If you were a non-smoker and a non-drinker back then when you had originally bought the term insurance, your risk profile would have been estimated differently compared to what your current risk profile is after developing those habits.
Good news: Your premium does not automatically change immediately based on the lifestyle habits you adopt. Term life policy gives you enough predictability and long-term affordability by locking in the premium determined for you at the start of the tenure (provided you pay it regularly). In other words, your current policy with the insurer remains intact without any modifications even after you have developed these habits.
What’s ideally recommended?: You are expected to inform the insurer about significant and major developments to your health or anything to do with your changed lifestyle, either during periodic medical reviews (as and when required), policy renewal or while making a claim to the company. It is considered to be an important responsibility at your end because if something happens to you and your untimely death gets connected to your lifestyle changes (smoking, drinking etc.), your family’s claim for the payout can be denied or delayed depending on the case and the investigation findings. Therefore, honesty is always the best policy when it comes to term insurance. In case you were a smoker / drinker right from the start but had informed the insurance company otherwise (during purchase of the policy), this amounts to non-disclosure (hiding of such crucial information) which again leads to several complications during the claim, most of them putting your family’s peace of mind, protection, finances and future at a huge risk. This, in turn, destroys the whole purpose of why you had purchased a term life policy in the first place.
In short: Being transparent at all times ensures that your family doesn’t have to go through any such stress later. Upon intimation of your newly-developed habits and health changes, your insurer may reassess your risk profile. You can also expect your premium to increase accordingly.
Why Should I Buy An Individual Term Life Insurance Plan When I Am Already Covered By My Employer’s Group Insurance?
Many companies often enable group life insurance plans for several of their employees working in the organization as a part of employee benefits. However, the most important question here is - Are group life insurance covers the same as personal term life insurance plans? Not really! While it is true that a company-provided group insurance is indeed valuable and comes with its own advantages, you can’t ignore the fact that it is directly linked to your employment with the organization. Meaning, when you exit from the organization, switch to another role or another company or if you choose to retire, then your coverage can be immensely impacted. Which is, when you actually need your policy to run the show, there is no surety that you will be covered under the corporate plan. The group cover ends with your employment in the organization, which is obviously not the case with your individual term insurance. Your policy stays active for the entire term you opted for as long as you pay the premium regularly without any lapses. Your individual term life policy has nothing to do with where you work, thus giving you complete control over the coverage.
Moreover, in an individual plan, you get to choose the sum assured for your family. Group plans only provide basic coverage and may not be sufficient enough for your specific needs and financial obligations. For instance, a corporate life cover plan may offer a Rs. 10-20 lakh coverage while your personal term insurance may actually provide a Rs. 1 crore sum assured or even Rs. 2 crore coverage based on your age, income level and financial responsibilities.
Along with your individual term life policy comes full flexibility to customize the coverage as per your choice and your family’s goals. You can choose a premium paying term that aligns with your cash flow situation, add riders to enhance the coverage for specific events like accidental death, critical illness etc., and even decide the policy term with the number of years you want your family to be covered under the plan. These features for personalization may not be available under the corporate plan which you are added to by your employer.
Having a workplace life cover is great, but it cannot be the only financial cushion you rely on for your family’s well-being tomorrow. An individual term life insurance means protection that is tailored, lifelong and sufficient enough, irrespective of where you are in your career.

Does My Term Life Policy Lapse If I Miss Or Stop My Premium Payments?
Regular premium payments
are an integral part of your term life insurance policy. Hence, failure to pay these premiums consistently (according to the premium paying term decided during the purchase of the policy), causes your term insurance coverage to become invalid and lapse. Not just that, your family loses the financial shield that you so painstakingly built for them to benefit from in the future in your absence. In case of untimely death of the policyholder during this lapsed period, the family has no right on the sum assured and the insurance company shall not be liable to pay any death benefit to them. Unlike other investment plans, the term insurance covers offer no maturity value i.e. if your policy lapses, you get nothing in return - no refund of the premiums paid till date (Want a pro tip to never miss a payment? You can set up an auto-debit! Alternatively you can also schedule alerts / reminders for these payments).
However, most insurance companies, including Ageas Federal Life Insurance, provide a grace period after the due date for policyholders who missed paying their premiums, so that you can at least have a second chance at retaining your coverage like before and nothing changes thereafter. Yet, if you still don’t clear the pending premium payment(s) within this window, then your policy is interrupted midway. And it lapses with no more benefits being rendered for your family.
In some cases, you may be allowed to reinstate or revive the policy, provided it is within a specific time frame (usually 2-5 years) as required by the insurer. This can be done by paying all outstanding premiums due till date (with interest) and by undergoing a new medical test if asked for by the company. So, even if you are dealing with any financial constraints, you can still restore the term life policy in this manner.
What Types Of Deaths Are Covered In Term Insurance Plans?
Term insurance plans generally cover most types of deaths. Natural causes of death and accidental deaths both.
What's included?
Natural causes: old age-related, health complications, listed illnesses etc.
Accidental causes: road / rail / airline accidents, occupational hazards - industrial incidents etc. (Benefit under base term insurance + Accidental death benefit rider if opted*)
Coverage for critical health issues like cancer, kidney failure and cardiac arrest: That is if these conditions were not pre-existing and undisclosed at the time of purchase. (Benefit under base term insurance + Critical illness rider if opted*)
Death due to homicide / assault (unless the insured / nominee found complicit in the crime)
Death outside India provided all relevant documents are submitted to the insurer during processing of the claim.
(*The policyholder has an option to add such a rider to enjoy add-on benefits and wider protection for specific cases.)
What’s not included?
There are also some exclusions you must be aware of before purchasing term insurance.
Death by suicide in the 1st year of the policy not covered. There’s a 12-month waiting period from the date of policy issuance.
Death due to participation in any illegal activities, self-inflicted injuries, or substance abuse excluded.
Non-disclosure of critical health information, incorrect age, misstatement of financials, hiding of medical conditions, and false information about lifestyle habits: If any of these cases are found upon investigation by the insurer, then the claim is rejected.
High-risk adventures (like mountaineering, private aviation, motor racing etc.) are not covered, unless informed to the insurance company during the purchase of the term life insurance plan.

Does Term Insurance Have A Maturity Benefit?
To know the answer to this question, you must firstly understand that the key factor which differentiates term insurance from a whole life insurance cover is - As a policyholder, what is your objective? Do you want only risk protection? Or do you want protection plus savings? Many experts recommend buying a term life insurance policy first and then adding more investment instruments to your kitty in order to build a wealth corpus. Each financial product comes with its own pros and cons. A pure term life insurance policy is only meant to provide protection to your family upon your death and it’s not designed to primarily help create wealth alongside. So, there is no maturity benefit that you get after the policy ends.
Answering the most common question every to-be policyholder has regarding term insurance - ‘Will I get my money back if I survive the policy term?’ Let us reiterate: No, there is no refund possible for any money paid - by way of premiums over the years - during the policy term in case you survive (unless you had opted for a term insurance with Return Of Premium variant at the time of purchase). Under this feature, you can get back all your premiums (minus the taxes and the rider cost) paid towards this term insurance plan. However, it is worthy to note that ROP plans are slightly costlier in comparison to the regular term insurance plans with no return of premium.
In traditional policies, there is no payout if the policyholder survives when the plan ends. If you still expect a lump sum when the policy matures, you can definitely go for a term insurance cover with Return Of Premium (ROP). For example, under a certain ROP plan, you pay Rs. 20,000 as annual premium for 30 years (policy term). At maturity, if you outlive this term, you may get back Rs. 6,00,000 as a lump sum. Remember, affordability is kicked out the moment you go for the ROP feature, as ROP plans can sharply push the premiums by 2X-3X. Also, the returns from ROP plans (example, say after 30 years policy duration) cannot be equated with the attractive returns from other investments / financial instruments like mutual funds, fixed deposits etc.
While ROP plans are ideal for those who do not prefer ‘no return’ policies, according to various experts, the combination of -
paying lower and affordable premiums for a traditional term insurance plan with a higher sum assured and
investing the extra amount (which you just saved by not opting for ROP) in some other financial tool like an SIP*
Generally speaking, it is a wiser financial decision to make, that can yield better returns in the long run.

Can I Change My Term Insurance Details Later During The Policy Tenure?
Certain details in your term insurance policy can be changed even at a later point in time like nominee information (updation / change possible anytime), contact information (phone number, email, address etc.), premium payment mode (move from monthly to quarterly, or half-yearly to annual or vice versa - switch as you like), extra riders (addition / removal possible at the time of renewal), and sum assured (top-up coverage). However, primary information like your age, lifestyle declarations (smoker status), health conditions (pre-existing) and occupation cannot be altered retrospectively. It is expected that you must provide correct and honest information transparently right at the time of purchase of term insurance.

Sample illustration: Your annual income is Rs. 12 lakh, and you have an outstanding home loan of Rs. 50 lakh. You think you may need Rs. 50 lakh in future to fund your child’s education abroad. In such a scenario, it is recommended that your coverage should ideally be around Rs. 1.5 crore to Rs. 2 crore for your family’s financial steadiness in your absence.
Can A Senior Citizen Buy Term Insurance?
The first step in a sound financial plan is buying term insurance. While starting early has many pros for you to seriously consider buying the top term life insurance cover, even senior citizens can purchase a term insurance policy. However, since age and health, both are not in your favour, entry into term life insurance is usually capped around 60-65 years. Some insurers extend this to even 70 years. In any case, when you start as a senior citizen in term life insurance plans, your coverage term reduces significantly and is much shorter compared to a young policyholder who enjoys long-term peace of mind. Your policy term can thus ideally range anywhere between 10-20 years. That’s not the only difference. Let’s talk about premiums. The cost of premium for a senior citizen is way more than what it is for a policyholder in their 20s, 30s and 40s. With old age, health risks go up enormously and the risk of mortality also increases for the individual. Considering this, the insurance company charges a relatively higher premium to compensate for the risk of claim that it undertakes in your specific case. Your eligibility and premium change depending on the critical health conditions you are suffering from.
So, as an older buyer of the term life insurance plan, here’s what’s in it for you -
Examples: A 62-year-old non-smoker applying for a Rs. 50 lakh term life policy may have to pay Rs. 85,000 - Rs. 1,10,000 as annual premium. The same cover for a 35-year-old non-smoker may cost only Rs. 8,000 - Rs. 12,000 per year.
A 65-year-old smoker may be charged Rs. 1.5 lakh to Rs. 2 lakh per year for a Rs. 50 lakh term life insurance policy (In some cases, they may be denied coverage altogether.) Whereas a 40-year-old smoker may still get the same cover for about Rs. 25,000 - Rs. Rs. 35,000 premium per year
A 65-year-old applying for term insurance may only get a policy that runs for 10 years (till age 75). Their application for a 30-year plan like someone in their 30s gets rejected.
Some insurers may allow coverage strictly till age 80 i.e. if a 62-year-old applies for a term insurance cover, they may be offered a policy with the duration of only 18 years, no matter how much they are willing to pay as premium towards the term life insurance plan.
Should you still consider buying a term plan if you are an older person?
Yes absolutely. Here’s why!
Older people buy term insurance to help family members with the repayment of any outstanding loans upon their death
If you have a person who is dependent financially on you, you can safeguard their financial journey with income replacement from the term insurance claim payout
A term life policy supports senior citizens in estate planning and legacy building as their heirs can manage wealth transfers smoothly with the tax-free lump sum. From enabling an option for debt-free inheritance to funding the future generations’ dreams, term life insurance for senior citizens can be viewed as a powerful tool here
Most senior citizens don’t have 30-40 years of income dependency. Also, there is a significantly higher claim probability in old age. So, a limited-term cover, even if it’s costlier, makes sense for many older buyers.
What are some alternative options for senior citizens?
Whole life insurance cover, pension plans, retirement annuities, and health and critical illness insurance.
Ageas Federal Life Insurance does provide term insurance to older individuals. Before buying a term plan, read the policy documents carefully for policy limitations, capped durations and maturity ages.
Is Term Insurance A Good Investment?
Ideally, in a traditional sense, term insurance cannot be looked upon as an investment option because if you outlive the policy term, you generally gain no returns from the term life policy. A term life insurance plan acts like a protection tool, helping to keep the financial stress of your loved ones away, when you are no longer around to safeguard their future.
Term life insurance: A financial cushion for the family upon the death of the life assured. Is that all to it? No. A term insurance cover is considered by industry experts as one of the best financial decisions you can ever make for your family because -
it aligns a huge sum assured with a really low premium
it guards your assets as your family need not be in a pressurized state later to sell their gold, property etc. in order to survive after your death
it even supports their financial journey ahead if they choose to invest the remaining funds elsewhere (like mutual funds) after paying off loans and other financial obligations
You can think of term insurance as a stepping stone to your future financial security, powered by a token of care (premium) committed consistently (premium paying term) over a predetermined period of time (policy term) for a large sum (coverage). In simple terms, a term life insurance plan is not an investment product. But the biggest return it promises (apart from the coverage amount after the insured person’s death) is the peace of mind throughout the policy term that if something miserable were to happen to your life, your family won’t be helpless and exposed, struggling for money. Your term life policy stands tall to guard them when you cannot!
Let's look at an example:
Saif is thinking of investing 1 lakh rupees every year towards an endowment policy.
Expert tip: Saif - instead of utilizing his entire budget / funds towards the endowment plan - should consider using Rs. 10,000 first, to pay as annual premium towards a term life policy. Remaining Rs. 90,000, if invested in mutual funds, over the next 20 years can yield better returns for Saif. This way, Saif can get dual benefits: Protection plus savings leading to wealth creation for his family, when they would need it the most.
What Is The Right Age To Buy Term Insurance?
Ideally, the perfect time to buy a term insurance cover is NOW. In other words, as early as you can. Because younger age = a healthier you, which means lower premiums and early lock-in benefits for your term life policy before possible health changes at a later point in time. For instance, at age 25, you may pay just half the premium compared to what you may have to pay in your 35 years of age in case you choose to start late. You can consider purchasing a term insurance cover even in your 20s as soon as you start earning. This ensures that your future plans (with respect to marriage, housing loan, children and retirement) are also secured as your commitments increase with age. Your application for term life insurance is more likely to be approved at a younger age due to easy eligibility and wider coverage without the fear of rejection due to medical reasons. So, to protect your people from uncertainties in future, buy a term plan today. The earlier, the better - even for a stress-free approval.
If you are a young earner and still don’t hold a term life policy, you can consider buying one around these milestones such as -
a huge salary jump,
marriage plans are around the corner,
you are taking big loans,
you are already a parent or planning for a child
you are in your 30s and considering early retirement (i.e. around 45 years of age)
you are a key stakeholder in any venture
you have elderly parents dependent on you
Are you ‘the sooner, the smarter’ kind of person when it comes to term life insurance? Kudos! You have indeed made a wise choice for yourself and your loved ones!
Does Smoking Affect Term Insurance Premiums?
Yes, 100%. Smoking has a direct impact on your term insurance premium. While applying for term insurance, you are expected to make some lifestyle declarations which include your smoking habits, alcohol consumption and tobacco chewing. These habits are proven to affect your health adversely, increasing your chances of respiratory conditions, heart diseases, cancer and other critical illnesses. Which means, the insurer's risk goes up when the probability of your claim rises. Hence, you have to pay a higher premium in such cases.
Term insurance premium for individuals with smoking habits is definitely on the higher side compared to the premium for non-smokers, even if the sum assured, policy term and other factors like applicant’s age are same.
Pawan, a 30-year-old non-smoker may have to pay Rs. 8,000 annually for a
Rs. 1 crore term insurance cover
Vikas, a 30-year-old smoker may have to pay as high as even Rs. 14,000 annually for the same
Rs. 1 crore term insurance plan
Key points to remember: Be honest. Smoking kills. Not just you, but also your family’s peace of mind during the claim. So if you think you can hide your smoking habit from your insurance form, you are absolutely wrong. When discovered later during investigation, the claim often stands rejected.
Smoking occasionally, smoking just a few times randomly, casual or social smoking - all such kinds of smoking are considered in insurance policy clauses. Do not be your own judge. Make these declarations upfront in your application.
In certain cases, you can be reclassified as non-smoker if you have quit the habit and have maintained the same status for a few years. Medical tests are required for confirmation.
Can I Buy Term Insurance Online Without A Medical Test?
Yes, you can buy term insurance online on several platforms that do not require you to undergo a medical test before applying. A medical test may not be necessarily mandated in those situations where the applicant is young, healthy, and has chosen a relatively smaller sum assured. In many such cases, an individual - who is supposedly in their 20s or early 30s, with a self-declared clear health status and a low-risk medical history running in the family - applies for a coverage ranging between Rs. 25 lakh and Rs. 50 lakh, and the insurance company may have no qualms in approving such an application, even without their medical test
On the other hand, let’s consider an example where the applicant is in their 40s, with lifestyle habits like smoking and drinking, opting for a higher coverage (Rs. 1 crore+). This applicant also has a family history of critical conditions. In such scenarios, the insurer almost always wants the applicant to undergo a medical check-up.
Advantages of ‘no medical test’ policies: Hassle-free experience, direct policy, quick approval, easy application process, minimal paperwork, no hospital visits, no waiting time - JUST CONVENIENCE
Disadvantages of ‘no medical test’ policies: Higher premiums, more risk for insurer - so lower cover amount, more chances of claim rejection
Pro tip: Medical tests provide better accuracy in policy issuance and premium determination. Even if your online term insurance doesn’t require you to go for a check-up, ensuring that you submit absolutely honest health information is critical during the application stage. Non-disclosure often leads to claim rejections later. For a hassle-free claim settlement, it is always recommended that you provide the insurer with all the necessary details and keep complete transparency regarding your health and medical history while buying any term insurance online. Otherwise, it can get extremely stressful for your family later, when you are no more, to claim the sum assured, because if the insurer discovers any mismatch in the information provided by you at the time of application - such as incorrect age, health problems, lifestyle disclosures and so on, the company can delay or deny payout. To secure peace of mind for your loved ones, maintain accurate information in every step of your term insurance journey - be it a medical test policy or a no medical test policy.
Why Does The Premium Increase With Age In Term Life Insurance?
When you are young, you are usually healthy, with no such critical conditions which generally affect your risk profile. However, as you age, your health status changes and deteriorates, increasing the chances of a claim when viewed from the insurer’s lens. Premiums are basically scientific calculations made on the basis of the relation between your age, health and coverage risk. Generally speaking, a 25-year-old individual, non-smoker, healthy male is viewed as less likely to pass away in the near term in comparison to a 50-year-old individual, diabetic, and social drinker. So, the key drivers for a higher premium are age, increased health risks, and shorter premium paying term. For example, 25-year-old Rahul may pay just Rs. 7,000 as annual premium towards a Rs. 1 crore term life policy. The same term insurance, in case Rahul starts later i.e when he turns 35 years - could cost Rs. 12,000. And if Rahul buys that term plan when he is 45 years old, the annual premium might just jump to anywhere between Rs. 25,000 and Rs. 30,000.
How Is The Premium For Term Insurance Calculated?
The calculation for a term insurance premium is done keeping in mind a mix of different parameters. The three main factors which are considered to calculate the premium for your term life insurance plan are your personal factors, your health & lifestyle factors and policy-related factors. This is done to understand your risk profile better and arrive at a suitable term life insurance, policy term and premium amount, which can work well for your specific budget, financial goals, life stages, family needs and your unique requirements for financial protection in the event of uncertainty.
Let’s look at twelve key factors which influence the calculation of premium for your term life policy: Age of the applicant: Your age is the most important component considered while deciding your premium.
The younger you are, the fewer health risks you face.
The earlier you start, the less you pay.
The sooner you buy a term insurance cover, the longer you stay protected.
Cover amount: If you are looking at a higher sum assured, then you may have to also pay a higher premium
Policy term: Longer policy duration = higher risk for the insurer = hence, higher premiums. However, you can benefit from the extended protection that you get for your loved ones i.e. coverage for more years, in turn aligning with your long-term goals for them.
Pre-existing health conditions: Existing health issues impact your premiums to a major extent as they increase the risk element for the insurance company.
Medical history: Your insurer assesses your health risks by examining your medical test reports and your family history of chronic and hereditary illnesses (diabetes, cancer, heart conditions etc.) to accordingly determine your premium. Even obesity causes your premium to increase.
Lifestyle habits: Your lifestyle habits influence your health, which in turn, impacts your term insurance premium. Submitting all details upfront regarding your smoking and drinking habits and high-risk hobbies like adventure sports lower the risk of rejection later.
Occupation: People in high-risk jobs like mining, construction and aviation, and those in professions involving higher levels of stress may have to also pay higher premiums. Moreover, your income level needs to align with your desired sum assured i.e. coverage amount expected. The insurance company may run a check to determine your occupational stability. High-stress professions too can increase your premium.
Extra riders: Choosing add-on riders to enhance protection increases your premium.
Gender: Generally speaking, as per statistics, women tend to live longer than men. Hence, if you are a woman applying for term insurance, your premiums are slightly lower for the same sum assured, in comparison to that of a man’s policy.
Mode of premium payment: Annual premiums are slightly cheaper compared to monthly installments and half-yearly premium payments.
Location: If you are residing in a high-risk geographical area (For example: highly polluted zones), then your premiums can be slightly costlier.
Underwriting results: Depending on the underwriting assessments, if any piece of information found in the medical reports or otherwise, puts you in a high-risk category for the insurer from a claim probability perspective, then the insurance company may choose to increase your premium or even reject your application altogether.
Premiums are not random figures. These are customized calculations made to ensure that your premium is adjusted to suit your life stage, type of term insurance, job risk, coverage amount, health issues, risk profile, lifestyle and choice of policy term.
