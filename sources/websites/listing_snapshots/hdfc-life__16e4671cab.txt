What is life insurance?
Life insurance is a contract between you and an insurance provider that offers financial protection to your family if you pass away during the policy period. In return for this protection, you pay regular premiums to keep the plan active. If something happens to the policyholder, the insurer pays a lump sum to the family, helping them manage expenses and maintain stability.
This amount can support everyday needs, future goals, and any financial responsibilities left behind. A life insurance plan is important because it gives your dependents security and confidence, even during difficult times. It acts as a long-term safety net, ensuring that your loved ones are taken care of when they need support the most.

Types of Life Insurance Plans
There are different types of life insurance, each designed to meet specific financial needs. These plans vary in how long they offer coverage, the benefits they provide, and the purpose they serve for your family’s future.
Understanding these types of life insurance
helps you choose a plan that aligns with your goals, whether it is protection, savings, or long-term financial planning.
1
1
Term Life Insurance
Term life insurance
offers high coverage at affordable premiums, making it one of the simplest forms of protection. It provides benefits only if the policyholder passes away during the policy term, ensuring financial security for dependents when they need it most. Since it does not include savings or investment components, it is ideal for those seeking pure financial protection and maximum coverage for a lower cost.
For instance, if you get a 1 crore term insurance plan for your family, they will be financially secured even after your untimely demise. It will be easier for them to pay for education, daily expenses, marriages and so on with the best term insurance plan for 1 crore
.
...Read More
2
2
Whole Life Insurance
A whole life insurance
plan covers the policyholder for their entire lifetime, offering long-term protection and financial certainty. Along with life cover, it builds savings over time, which can be useful for future needs. This combination makes it suitable for individuals who want lifelong financial security for their dependents. It ensures that family members receive support whenever an eventuality occurs, regardless of when it happens.
...Read More
3
3
Endowment Plans
Endowment policies
combine insurance protection with disciplined savings. They pay a maturity benefit if the policyholder survives the policy term, along with a life cover throughout the duration.
This makes them suitable for long-term financial goals such as education, milestones, or wealth building. The structured savings element helps individuals stay committed to their financial plans while also ensuring security for their families.

Benefits of Life Insurance Plans
Life insurance plans offer benefits such as financial security, long-term planning, and peace of mind. While each type of plan offers unique advantages, they generally help families maintain financial stability during unexpected events, build savings, and prepare for future needs. These benefits highlight the importance of life insurance

Financial Protection for Family
Life insurance provides a strong financial safety net for dependents in case of an unexpected event. The payout helps cover essential expenses, maintain daily living standards, and manage future needs. This protection ensures that families remain secure during difficult times, making it the core purpose of life insurance plans .
Income Replacement
Life insurance can act as a substitute for lost income if the policyholder passes away. The benefit amount helps the family continue meeting monthly commitments such as rent, bills, education costs, or household expenses. Supporting everyday cash flow, it offers stability during periods of financial uncertainty.
Wealth Creation and Savings
Certain life insurance plans offer long-term savings or investment growth alongside protection. These plans help policyholders build a financial corpus over time, making them suitable for future goals such as education, retirement plans, or wealth creation. They encourage consistent savings while providing the added advantage of life cover.
Tax Benefits
Life insurance premiums may qualify for tax deductions under Section 123 of the Income Tax Act, 2025 1 (corresponding to Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, 1961), subject to overall limit of Rs. 1.5 lakh per tax year helping individuals reduce their taxable income. Also, deductions under Section 126 of the Income Tax Act, 2025 1 (corresponding to Section 80D of the Income Tax Act, 1961) are applicable for term insurance policy premiums when the policy includes health-related riders such as critical illness, hospital care or disability, with the deduction limits being ₹25,000 for self, spouse, and dependent children, and if the individual is a senior citizen, the limit increases up to ₹50,000.
Additionally, an additional ₹25,000 can be claimed for premiums paid for parents, and this limit increases to ₹50,000 if the parents are senior citizens (above 60 years), making maximum deduction upto ₹1 lakh in a tax year. Additionally, regular payouts received from a Life Insurance policy are exempt under Section 11 read with Schedule II of the Income Tax Act, 2025 1 (corresponding to Section 10(10D) of the Income Tax Act, 1961), subject to the policy satisfies prescribed conditions, including the premium-to-sum-assured ratio to not exceed 10% of the sum-assured and aggregate annual premium is not exceeding ₹2,50,000 for ULIPs and ₹5,00,000 for Non-ULIPs. These tax advantages make life insurance a beneficial part of financial planning, while keeping the overall process simple and compliant with regulations.
Note: If assessee has opted for Old tax regime, assessee shall be eligible to claim deduction under chapter VIII of the Income Tax Act, 2025 (like sections 123, 126, etc.) [corresponding to Chapter VI-A (like Section 80C, 80D, 80CCC, etc) of the Income Tax Act, 1961]. If assessee opted for New tax regime only few deductions under Chapter VIII such as Sections 146, 124, 125 and 147 of the Income Tax Act, 2025 (corresponding to Section 80JJAA, 80CCD(2), 80CCH(2) and 80LA of the Income Tax Act, 1961) are available.

Why Is Life Insurance a Safe Investment?
Life insurance is considered a safe and low-risk financial product because it offers protection, regardless of how the market performs. Unlike market-linked investments, the life cover remains intact throughout the policy term, ensuring your family receives financial support if something unexpected happens. Many traditional plans, such as endowment plans or whole life policies, also provide assured returns or maturity benefits, giving you clarity about what you will receive in the future.
This predictability makes life insurance plans a stable option for long-term planning. You know the coverage amount, premium schedule, and expected outcomes, which helps in building financial discipline. Life insurance offers a dual advantage: risk-free protection for your family and steady savings that grow over time.
While it may not provide high market-linked returns, it ensures security, stability, and peace of mind, making it one of the safest components of a balanced financial plan.
How Does Life Insurance Work?
Life insurance works through a contract between the policyholder and the insurer. The policyholder pays regular premiums to keep the plan active. In return, the insurer provides financial protection, offering a lump-sum payout to the family if the policyholder passes away during the policy term. This simple system ensures long-term security for dependents.
01
Application Process
The life insurance application process begins with submitting your personal, financial, and contact details to the insurer. You must provide KYC documents such as identity and address proof. Depending on the plan and your age or health, a medical test may also be required. Sharing accurate information helps the insurer assesses your profile accurately, leading to faster approval and smoother policy issuance.
02
Underwriting and Risk Assessment
Once your application is submitted, the insurer reviews key factors such as your age, health history, lifestyle habits, and financial details. This evaluation is known as underwriting. Based on this assessment, the insurer determines your eligibility for the life insurance plan and calculates the premium you need to pay. The process ensures that coverage and pricing are fair and aligned with your risk profile.
03
Premium Payments Explained
Policyholders can pay premiums monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, yearly, or as a one-time single premium, depending on the plan. The premium amount is influenced by factors like age, the coverage chosen, and overall risk profile. Paying premiums on time is essential because it keeps the policy active and ensures continuous financial protection for your family.
04
Inclusions and Exclusions
Life insurance plans generally cover natural death, accidental death, and other events specified in the plan. However, they also include exclusions, such as suicide within the initial policy years, fraud, or non-disclosure of important information.
Each plan may have its own set of inclusions and limitations. It is important to read the policy document carefully to understand what is covered and what is not before purchasing.
05
How Death Benefit Payout Works
When the policyholder passes away, the nominee must file a claim by submitting required documents such as the claim form, death certificate, and identity proof. Once the insurer receives these details, they review the information, verify the policy status, and assess whether all terms and conditions have been met.
After successful verification, the insurer processes the death benefit. Depending on the policies chosen, the payout may be provided as a lump sum, regular income, or a combination of both, ensuring timely financial support for the family.

Factors That Affect Life Insurance Premiums
Life insurance premiums differ for each person because insurers assess risk based on age, health, lifestyle, and occupation. Policy factors like plan type, coverage amount, and duration also influence the cost. Health history and existing conditions further affect the final premium.
Understanding the following factors that help you choose a life insurance pla n is essential:
Age
Younger applicants generally enjoy lower premiums because they pose lower health risks. As age increases, the chances of medical issues rise, resulting in higher premium costs. That is why buying insurance early is one of the smartest financial decisions.
Gender
Premium rates may vary slightly based on life expectancy trends. Women often pay lower premiums because statistically, they tend to live longer. This difference is minor but still influences the final cost of the policy.
Health History
Your medical background plays a major role in premium calculation. Pre-existing illnesses or chronic conditions can increase your premium because they imply a higher risk. Healthy individuals usually receive better rates, and medical tests during underwriting can affect the final pricing.
Lifestyle
Lifestyle choices directly impact your premium. Habits like smoking or frequent alcohol consumption raise health risks, leading to higher premiums for your life insurance plan . On the other hand, an active and balanced lifestyle can lower risk levels. Insurers also verify honesty in lifestyle disclosures during underwriting.
Occupation
Your job can influence your insurance cost. High-risk professions, such as mining, aviation, or construction, may attract higher premiums due to increased risk. Low-risk, desk-based jobs usually qualify for lower premium rates because the risk of accidental harm is minimal.
Sum Assured
The amount of coverage you choose significantly affects the premium you pay. Higher protection levels naturally result in higher premiums. It is important to select a sum assured that aligns with your financial responsibilities, as the premium varies depending on the level of coverage you need.
Life Insurance Plan Riders
Riders* are optional add-ons that enhance your life insurance policy by offering extra protection beyond the basic coverage. They allow you to customise your plan based on specific risks or personal needs, ensuring more comprehensive financial security.
You can strengthen your policy and make it better aligned with your life goals and circumstances with the following riders:
Accidental Death Benefit Rider
This rider offers an additional payout if the policyholder’s death occurs due to an accident, providing enhanced financial security for dependents. It is particularly suitable for individuals who travel frequently or work in environments with higher risk exposure. Families receive extra support by adding this rider during unexpected situations, ensuring greater stability and protection.
Waiver of Premium Rider
With this rider, all future premiums are waived if the policyholder experiences a disability or loss of income. This ensures the life insurance policy continues without creating any financial burden. It is useful for maintaining long-term protection, as the policy remains active even when the policyholder is unable to pay premiums due to unforeseen circumstances.
Critical Illness Rider*
The critical illness rider provides a lump sum payout upon the diagnosis of major illnesses. This amount can help cover medical treatments, recovery costs, or lifestyle adjustments during a challenging phase. It is ideal for those seeking added financial protection against sudden medical emergencies that could strain their savings or long-term plans.
Accelerated Death Benefit Rider*
This rider pays a portion of the sum assured if the policyholder is diagnosed with a terminal illness. It offers financial support for medical or personal expenses during critical times, helping ease immediate burdens. However, the amount paid in advance reduces the final sum payable to beneficiaries, ensuring financial support is available when needed the most.
Long-Term Care Rider*
The long-term care rider offers financial assistance for prolonged medical or assisted-care needs. It helps manage the cost of chronic conditions or age-related limitations that require ongoing support. This rider is especially beneficial for ageing individuals or those at higher risk of long-term health challenges.
Child Term Rider*
A child term rider provides life cover for the insured child, offering financial protection in the event of an unforeseen loss. It helps families manage emotional and financial strain while ensuring long-term stability. This rider strengthens family security by extending coverage to children during vulnerable years.

Types of Death Not Covered by Life Insurance
Life insurance plans include certain exclusions under which claims may not be paid. Here are the different types of death not covered in life insurance plans:
Suicide in the First Policy Year
In case of death due to suicide within 12 months from the date of commencement of risk under the policy or from the date of revival of the policy, as applicable, the nominee or beneficiary of the policyholder shall be entitled to at least 80% of the total premiums paid till the date of death or the surrender value available as on the date of death whichever is higher, provided the policy is in force.Fraud, Misrepresentation, or Intentional Self-Harm
If the policyholder provides false information, hides medical conditions, or engages in intentional self-harm, the claim may be rejected. Therefore, accurate disclosures are essential.
Illegal or Criminal Activities
Deaths occurring while participating in unlawful activities are generally excluded from coverage. Such activities include theft, trespassing, or violent acts.
Drug or Alcohol Abuse
If death results from substance abuse, overdose, or excessive alcohol consumption, insurers may deny the claim as it falls under high-risk behaviour.
Hazardous Activities Not Disclosed
Risky hobbies such as skydiving, racing, or mountaineering may be excluded if not declared during the application.
War, Riots, or Terrorism
Some plans exclude deaths caused during war, riots, civil unrest, or terrorist activities.
Note: Exclusions vary by insurer, so reading the policy document carefully is essential.

8
What happens if I stop paying my life insurance premiums?
If premiums are not paid, the policy may lapse or lose its benefits. For some plans, you may have the option to convert them to a paid-up policy. In this case, you will get reduced benefits based on the premiums already paid. Moreover, non-payment of premiums can affect coverage and may reduce or eliminate the financial protection intended for your family.
9
How can I file a life insurance claim?
Life insurance claims can be filed online, through customer support, or at the insurer’s branch. The nominee must submit a completed claim form, the policy document, death certificate, proof of identity, and any supporting medical or hospital records. The insurer verifies the documents and processes the payout. Therefore, following the proper steps ensures timely and smooth claim settlement for the beneficiaries.
10
What do you mean by paid-up value in life insurance?
Paid-up value is the reduced sum assured that a life insurance policy provides when premiums are stopped before the policy matures, but it remains active. It is calculated based on the premiums paid until that point. While the coverage is lower than the original policy, the paid-up option allows continued protection without additional payments.
11
Can I withdraw money from my life insurance policy?
Some life insurance policies, such as endowment or ULIPs, allow partial withdrawals or loans against the policy. This provides liquidity for emergencies or important expenses. Usually, term plans do not offer withdrawal options as they focus purely on protection. Therefore, always check your policy terms to understand the withdrawal rules and any potential impact on benefits.
12
What happens if I outlive my life insurance policy term?
If you outlive a term life insurance policy, the coverage ends, and no death benefit is paid. Some plans may offer maturity benefits or the option to renew for an extended term. This is why reviewing your coverage periodically and choosing the best life insurance plan is important to ensure ongoing protection for your family.
13
Should you buy more than one life insurance policy?
Buying multiple best life insurance policies can be beneficial if your financial responsibilities exceed the sum assured of a single plan. It can provide higher protection, diversify benefits, and cover different life stages. However, managing multiple policies requires careful planning to avoid overlaps, excessive premiums, or complicated claim processes. Assess your total financial needs before buying multiple plans.
