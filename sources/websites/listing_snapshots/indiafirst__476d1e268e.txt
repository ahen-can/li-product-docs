What is a Life Insurance Policy?
A life insurance plan is an agreement between an insurance company and an individual. The insurance company agrees to pay a lump sum to one or more beneficiaries in the event of the insured person’s passing during the policy term. In return, the policyholder pays a fixed amount at regular intervals (called the premium) to keep the policy active.
Today, you will find different types of life insurance plans online. Some focus on pure protection, such as term insurance. Others focus on financial returns, such as ULIPs (which offer investment-linked growth and guaranteed returns) and savings plans (which offer fixed growth). With multiple options in the market, it is essential to be clear on what life insurance is and what your needs are before choosing the right life insurance plan.

What are the Benefits of Life Insurance?
After looking through its types, let’s get familiar with the benefits of life insurance
:
Coverage against Risk
The life insurance death benefit payout is the most important aspect of a policy. It provides financial protection against the risk of the insured passing away. It helps the family manage daily expenses, outstanding loans, and other financial responsibilities in the absence of the insured.
Growth of Funds
Market-linked life insurance plans, such as ULIPs, allow a portion of the premium to be invested in equity, debt, or balanced funds. Over time, this investment component helps grow wealth and supports long-term financial goals.
Coverage for Increasing Expenses
Life insurance policies in India often come with additional features such as riders, coverage increases, or premium waiver benefits. The features help you prepare for rising expenses, unexpected medical emergencies, or sudden financial needs that may arise in the future.
Guaranteed Returns
Certain life insurance plans focus on savings and offer guaranteed benefits. The returns are decided at the time of buying the policy and are not affected by market changes. Such plans help you plan future expenses with confidence and financial stability.
Support During Critical Life Stages
Life insurance plans can support important life stages such as marriage, home purchase, children’s education, or retirement. The payouts or savings that you build through the policy can help manage expenses during these milestones.
Long-Term Savings Discipline
Paying regular premiums encourages a habit of disciplined saving. Over time, the habit can help you reach your financial goals while ensuring that your life insurance coverage continues without interruption.
Peace of Mind
Knowing that your family’s financial future is protected provides peace of mind. The assurance allows you to focus on your work and personal life without constant worry about unexpected financial challenges.
Tax Savings
Premiums paid toward a life insurance policy can be eligible for tax deductions under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act
. The benefits received from the policy may also qualify for tax exemptions, subject to prevailing rules and regulations. **
Income Replacement for Family
Certain life insurance plans offer payouts in the form of regular income rather than a lump sum. They help replace the loss of income and allow the family to manage monthly expenses more easily.
Loan Facility
With certain insurers, you can take a loan against the life insurance policy value. The feature can help you meet urgent financial needs, such as medical expenses or short-term requirements, without surrendering your plan.

How Does a Life Insurance Policy Work?
A life insurance policy is designed to give financial protection to your family. It works on a simple idea: you pay a small amount regularly, and in return, your family receives financial support if something happens to you.
Here’s how a life insurance plan works
, step by step:
Customer’s specifications
First, you choose a life insurance plan based on your needs. It could be a term life insurance plan, a savings plan, an endowment plan, or a ULIP. You also decide the coverage amount and select a nominee who will receive the payout.
Customer’s monetary input
To keep the life insurance policy active, you need to pay premiums regularly: monthly, quarterly, or yearly. If you are wondering what the insurance premium is
, it is simply the amount you pay to continue your life insurance coverage.
Legally valid layer of financial protection
As long as you pay the premium on time, your life insurance plan stays active. During this period, your family remains financially protected. Some plans may also offer savings, returns, or investment benefits.
Policy’s monetary output
If the policyholder passes away during the policy term, the nominee can file a claim with the insurance company. After verifying the claim, the insurer pays the death benefit as mentioned in the life insurance policy.
Variation by Policy Type
In term insurance
, the payout is given only if the policyholder passes away during the policy term. In certain types of plans, like an endowment plan or guaranteed return plans, the policyholder may receive a maturity payout if they survive the policy term. Each plan has its own benefits, inclusions, and exclusions.
In simple words, you pay a small amount today so that your family receives financial support in the future. It is one of the key reasons why choosing the best life insurance policy in India is an important financial decision.
What are Different Types of Life Insurance Policies?
There are several types of life insurance plans
in the market, with each one offering a unique feature to suit the differing needs of customers.
Broadly, life insurance can be classified into four categories:
Participating (par) Plans
These life insurance plans usually have a minimum guaranteed amount payable either upon death or maturity. In addition to this guaranteed amount, the policyholder may receive bonuses declared by the insurer from time to time. Once declared, these bonuses are added to the policy benefits and remain guaranteed.
In short, their primary purpose is to ensure guaranteed benefits with bonuses, and they’re suitable for long-term savers seeking added returns.
Non-participating (non-par) Plans
They are also known as term plans. They are pure protection plans and can span 99 to 100 years. They work in a simple manner: get life coverage in exchange for a premium to be paid every year. Non-par plans are usually the most affordable.
In short, their primary purpose is to ensure pure life protection, and they’re suitable for Individuals needing affordable, high coverage.
Non-participating Savings Plans
They are savings-oriented life insurance products that offer benefits guaranteed in absolute terms right at the start of the policy. The insurer bears the risk related to returns and guarantees. Non-participating savings plans are suitable for individuals looking for predictable returns and financial stability.
In short, their primary purpose is to ensure guaranteed savings, and they’re suitable for risk-averse individuals.
Unit Linked Insurance Plans (ULIPs)
They offer a combination of investment and protection benefits. A portion of the premium goes towards life cover, while the remaining amount is invested in market-linked funds. ULIP policyholders can choose asset classes such as equity, debt, or balanced funds, based on their risk appetite and financial goals.
In short, their primary purpose is to ensure investment with protection, and they’re suitable for investors with long-term goals.

Who Can Buy a Life Insurance Policy in India?
Anyone who understands the importance of future financial risks and wishes to secure their loved ones should purchase a life insurance policy.
Life insurance is not limited to a specific age or life stage. It can be useful at different points in life for different reasons.
A person can also purchase a long-term insurance policy for life. Regardless, the following profiles may find a life insurance policy more suitable:
Anyone with Financial Responsibilities
If you have people depending on you, such as a spouse, children, or parents, a life insurance policy helps ensure they are financially secure even if something unexpected happens.
Early Career Adults
Young professionals (for example, those in their 20s) can buy a policy to protect their family early on. It could help cover debts, daily expenses, and future goals, while you pay lower premiums.
Mid-life Individuals
If you are in your 30s or beyond and have growing responsibilities, such as school fees, house loans, or monthly expenses, a life insurance policy can act as long-term financial support.
People Planning for Long-Term Goals
Life insurance plans are not only about protection. Some plans help build savings or work as an investment plan
for goals like retirement or creating wealth over time.
Those With Different Life Situations
You can still buy a policy even if you:
Are not a permanent Indian citizen
Smoke or use tobacco
Have a medical condition
Have a disability
In such cases, the detailed terms, such as premiums, sum assured, and eligibility, will be decided based on your personal profile. The particulars are checked by the insurer to offer the most suitable coverage.
Tip: One can use an online life insurance calculator
to understand the particulars of a policy. The tool takes in basic personal information of the life insured, along with the selected policy features, to provide estimates of the premium payments.

How To Claim Benefits of Life Insurance Plans in India?
Filing a life insurance claim
allows the nominee or beneficiary to receive their rightful dues at the right time. While the exact steps may vary slightly across insurers, the overall claim process is simple.
Inform the Insurance Company
The first step is to inform the insurance company about the claim as soon as possible. It can usually be done online, through customer care, or by visiting the insurer’s branch.
Submit the Claim Intimation Form
Once the claim is informed, the nominee or beneficiary needs to submit the official claim intimation or claim form. It can be done digitally or in physical form, depending on the insurer’s process.
Claim Settlement and Payout
Once the claim is approved, the insurer releases the claim amount to the nominee or beneficiary through the chosen payout mode (usually via bank transfer).
Claim Verification by the Insurer
After receiving the documents, the insurance company verifies the details and assesses the claim as per the policy terms and conditions. Additional documents may be requested if required.
Provide the Required Documents
All necessary documents, such as the life insurance policy details, death certificate, and identity proof of the claimant, must be submitted.

What are the Basic Life Insurance Terminology?
Answer
In order to understand life insurance policy details and buy life insurance, there are a few basic life insurance terms you should know.
Policyholder – When you buy life insurance and make periodic life insurance premium payments, you become a life insurance policyholder. You can own the policy and also assign someone else as the life assured within the policy.
Life assured – The insured person who has the life cover as mentioned in the life insurance policy details. If this individual passes away, the concerned persons can raise a claim.
Life insurance premium – The amount you pay to buy life insurance and keep the policy active.
Sum assured – The guaranteed sum of money that your nominees/beneficiaries will receive in case of the life insurance policyholder's demise.
Beneficiary - A beneficiary is the person who ultimately receives the payout from the life insurance plan. In many cases, the nominee and beneficiary are the same.
Death benefit – It is the amount paid to the nominee if the policyholder passes away during the policy term. The death benefit and the sum assured are not the same. The death benefit is likely to be higher than the sum assured as it includes rider benefits and bonuses (if any).
Survival Benefit - A survival benefit is paid to the policyholder upon completing a pre-determined amount of life policy tenure.
Maturity benefit – A maturity benefit is paid when the insured outlasts the life insurance policy term. Pure protection or term life insurance plans do not offer survival or maturity benefits.
Riders – Riders are additional features that can be added to life insurance plans for a fee to expand the scope of the basic life cover policy. Typical life insurance rider options include accidental death benefit rider, accidental total and permanent disability benefit, critical illness cover, and waiver of life insurance premium rider.
Nominee – A nominee is the person you choose to receive the policy benefits in case of your death. It is important to keep nominee details updated to ensure smooth claim settlement.
Policy Term - The policy term is the duration for which the life insurance policy remains active. Coverage is available only during this period.
Grace Period - It is the extra time given after the premium due date in order to make the payment without letting the policy lapse. Coverage usually continues during the grace period.
Lapse - A policy is said to lapse if premiums are not paid within the grace period. A lapsed policy does not provide coverage unless revived.
Revival - It refers to the restoration of a lapsed life insurance policy by paying pending premiums and meeting insurer conditions.
Exclusions - They are situations or conditions for which the insurer does not pay a claim. They are clearly mentioned in the policy document.
Free Look Period - It is a short review period after buying the policy, during which you can cancel if you are not satisfied, subject to terms.
Surrender Value - If you exit a savings-based life insurance plan before maturity, the amount you receive is called the surrender value.
Claim Settlement - It is the process through which the insurer pays the policy benefits to the nominee or beneficiary.
Understanding these terms makes it easier to manage your life insurance plan with confidence.

What Happens If There Is No Nominee or the Nominee Has Passed Away?
Answer
If there is no nominee mentioned in the life insurance policy, or if the nominee has passed away before the policyholder, the claim amount does not get cancelled. Instead, it is paid to the legal heirs of the policyholder.
In such cases:
Legal heirs need to submit proof of relationship with the policyholder
Documents such as a legal heir certificate, succession certificate, or court order may be required
The insurer verifies the legal documents before processing the claim
If multiple legal heirs exist, the claim amount may be given out as per existing laws or court directions.
It is also possible for the policyholder to update or change the nominee during the life insurance policy term. By keeping nominee details updated, the policyholder can avoid delays and unnecessary legal processes during claim settlement.
In situations where both the nominee and the legal heirs are unclear, the insurer may wait for legal clarification. As a result, nomination and regular policy updates are extremely important.

A nominee who passes away, will by default be ineligible to claim any entitled benefits from the policy. In such a situation, the policyholder can change the nominee. The details of this process and the subsequent changes and impact on the policy benefits can be discussed with the insurance company and/or an insurance policy expert.
What Are the Types of Riders Available in a Life Insurance Policy?
Answer
Life insurance riders
are additional benefits you can add to your life insurance policy by paying a small extra premium. The riders help customise your plan and provide extra protection based on your personal needs and life stage.
Instead of buying separate policies, you can add riders and enhance coverage under one plan.
Here are some common types of riders available with life insurance plans:
Critical Illness
The rider provides financial support if the insured individual is diagnosed with a listed critical illness, such as cancer, heart attack, or kidney failure. The payout can be used for treatment costs, recovery expenses, or loss of income during the illness.
Accidental Death Benefit
The rider offers an additional payout if the policyholder dies due to an accident. The extra amount is paid over and above the base life insurance cover, to help the family manage sudden financial needs.
Long-Term Care
The rider helps cover expenses related to long-term medical care arising from serious illness, injury, or disability. It can be useful when continuous care or assistance is required over an extended period.
Accelerated Death Benefit
With this rider, a portion of the life insurance cover is paid in advance if the insured individual is diagnosed with a terminal illness. The amount can help manage medical expenses or other financial needs while the policyholder is still alive.
Waiver of Premium
If the insured individual becomes disabled or critically ill, this rider allows future premiums to be waived. The life insurance policy continues to remain active without additional payments.
What are the Factors That Affect Life Insurance Premiums?
Answer
A life insurance premium is the amount you pay to keep your life insurance policy active. The premium is decided after evaluating several personal, financial, and policy-related factors. Understanding these factors is important, as it helps you choose a life insurance plan that offers the right balance between coverage and costs.
Age of the Policyholder
Age is one of the most important factors affecting life insurance premiums
. Younger individuals usually pay lower premiums because they are considered less risky to insure. Buying a life insurance plan early helps lock in affordable premiums for a longer duration.
Health Condition and Medical History
Your current health and past medical records play a major role in determining the premium. Pre-existing conditions, chronic illnesses, or serious health issues may lead to higher premiums. In some cases, medical tests may be required before issuing a life insurance policy.
Lifestyle Habits
Lifestyle choices such as smoking, tobacco use, or excessive alcohol consumption, can increase life insurance premiums. The habits raise health risks, which insurers consider while calculating the premium amount.
Occupation and Job Risk
Your profession also impacts your premium. Individuals working in high-risk jobs such as mining, construction, aviation, or chemical industries, may be charged higher premiums due to the increased risk of injury or death.
Coverage Amount (Sum Assured)
The higher the life insurance cover you choose, the higher the premium you need to pay. Selecting an adequate but realistic sum assured ensures proper financial protection without going over your budget.
Policy Term
The length of the policy term affects the premium amount. Longer policy terms usually come with lower annual premiums when compared to short-term policies with the same coverage amount.
Type of Life Insurance Plan
Different types of life insurance come with different premium structures. Term insurance generally has lower premiums, while savings plans, whole life insurance
, or unit linked insurance plans may have higher premiums.
Riders and Add-ons
Adding riders such as critical illness, accidental death benefit, or waiver of premium increases the overall premium value. However, they provide extra protection and may reduce the need for separate policies.
Premium Payment Frequency
The frequency of premium payment—monthly, quarterly, or annually—also affects the total cost. Annual payments are usually more cost-effective than instalments.
Gender
In some cases, women may be charged lower premiums than men, due to higher life expectancy. It depends on the insurer’s underwriting rules.
Insurer’s Underwriting Guidelines
Each insurance company follows its own risk assessment procedure and underwriting guidelines. It is why premiums for the same life insurance cover may vary across insurers.
Understanding these factors can help you choose the best life insurance policy that suits your needs while remaining affordable in the long run.
What are the Payout Options Available in Life Insurance?
Answer
Life insurance plans offer different payout options to suit varied financial needs.
Common payout options include:
Lump sum payout, where the entire benefit amount is paid at once to the nominee or policyholder
Regular income payout, which provides monthly or annual income for a fixed period
Combination payout, where part of the amount is paid as a lump sum and the rest as regular income
Deferred payout, commonly seen in pension or retirement plans
Choosing the right payout option helps ensure better financial planning and long-term stability for your family.
How to File a Maturity Claim for a Life Insurance Policy?
Answer
A maturity claim is paid when the policyholder survives till the end of the life insurance policy term.
The process usually goes in the following manner:
The insurer informs the policyholder about maturity in advance
Submission of maturity claim form and policy document
Providing bank account details for payout
Verification of documents by the insurer
Payment of the maturity benefit to the policyholder
Maturity claims tend to be simpler and faster when compared to death benefit claims.
What are the Types of Death Not Covered in Life Insurance?
Answer
While life insurance plans offer wide protection, certain types of death may not be covered, especially if they occur under specific conditions.
Common exclusions include:
Death due to suicide within the initial policy period
Death caused by participation in illegal activities
Death due to self-inflicted injuries
Death caused by war, riots, or civil unrest (as specified in policy terms)
Death occurring under the influence of drugs or alcohol, in some cases.
Policy exclusions are clearly mentioned in the life insurance policy document and should be reviewed carefully.

How to Save Tax on a Life Insurance Policy?
Answer
A life insurance policy also provides an effective way to save tax. Many life insurance plans in India offer tax benefits at different stages—while paying premiums, during the policy term, and at the time of payout. Understanding how these tax benefits work can help you make better financial decisions and reduce your overall tax burden.
Below are the key ways in which you can save tax using a life insurance plan.
Tax Benefits on Life Insurance Premiums (Section 80C)
Premiums paid towards a life insurance policy are eligible for tax deductions under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, 1961. You can claim deductions of up to ₹1.5 lakh in a financial year, subject to the overall Section 80C limits.
The benefit is available with:
Term life insurance plans
Endowment plans
ULIPs ( Unit Linked Insurance Plans
)
Whole life insurance plans
Child insurance and savings plans
To qualify, the premium amount should generally not exceed a specified percentage of the sum assured, as per prevailing tax rules.
Tax-Free Maturity and Death Benefits (Section 10(10D))
One of the biggest tax advantages of a life insurance policy is that the payouts are usually tax-free under Section 10(10D).
It applies to:
Death benefits paid to the nominee
Maturity benefits received at the end of the policy term
Survival benefits paid during the policy term
The maturity and survival benefits are exempt from income tax only if the policy meets certain conditions related to premium limits.
Tax Savings Through ULIPs and Investment Plans
ULIPs combine insurance and investment, which makes them suitable for long-term wealth creation and tax planning. Premiums paid for a ULIP qualify for deductions under Section 80C, while maturity proceeds are usually tax-free under Section 10(10D)
.
ULIPs allow you to invest in equity or debt funds, on the basis of your risk appetite. Since switching between funds within a ULIP is usually tax-free, they also offer flexibility without immediate tax impact.
Additional Tax Benefits with Riders
Many life insurance policies allow you to add riders such as critical illness or accidental death benefit. Premiums paid for health-related riders may qualify for tax deductions under Section 80D
, subject to applicable limits. You can enhance your coverage while also increasing your tax-saving potential, without buying a separate policy.
Tax Benefits on Pension and Retirement Plans
Life-insurance–based pension plans and annuity plans also offer tax advantages. Premiums paid towards certain pension plans qualify for deductions under Section 80C.
At vesting, a portion of the retirement corpus can usually be withdrawn as a lump sum, which may be tax-free as per current rules, while the remaining amount is used to purchase an annuity. It helps in planning both retirement income and tax efficiency.
Tax Benefits for NRIs
Life insurance for NRIs
purchased in India also offers tax benefits under Indian tax laws. Premiums paid and benefits received are eligible for tax deductions and exemptions, subject to applicable conditions. It makes life insurance in India a useful tool for NRIs looking to secure their family and optimise taxes.
When claiming tax benefits, remember to
Always disclose correct personal and financial details
Keep premium payment receipts safely for tax filing
Check policy eligibility under current tax laws
Make a note of any changes to the Tax rules and then review the related benefits periodically.
Will I have to pay tax on a life insurance policys maturity benefit?
Answer
No. The maturity benefit is tax-free, courtesy of Section 10(10D) of the Income Tax Act of 1961. Do note that tax laws are subject to change with time, and you will have to keep updating yourself with the latest information in order to make the best use of your policy.
What is the meaning of paid-up value in life insurance?
Answer
After a certain period (say 3 to 5 years), upon failure to make the premium payments, a reduced sum assured amount is paid out instead of the actual pre-decided amount at the time of purchase. The reduced amount is said to be a ‘paid-up value’. Upon the passing away of the life insured, for no premium payments made beyond a certain year, the paid-up value is offered to the nominee and not the original sum assured value.
