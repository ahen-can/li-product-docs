What is an Endowment Plan?
An endowment plan is a type of life insurance policy
which offers life cover by paying the death benefit to the nominees of the policy in the event of death of the insured and also provides savings or investment components which can be utilized during the term of the policy or upon maturity. Endowment plans offer guaranteed returns which may be in the form of death benefit on the death of the insured during the term of the plan or in the form of maturity benefit if the life insured survives the term of the plan 2 .
How Does an Endowment Plan in India Work?
Endowment plans are both an insurance plan and a savings instrument. A portion of the premiums of the endowment plans goes towards life insurance cover
and the remaining portion goes towards savings or is invested to generate returns over the tenure of the policy. Endowment plans provide either of the two benefits:
Death benefit: which is the sum assured paid to the nominee on the insured’s death during the policy term, or
Maturity benefit: is a lump sum amount paid on maturity along with accrued bonuses, if any, upon the insured’s survival till the end of policy tenure 2 .
The death benefit ensures financial security of the family in times of crisis and the maturity benefit enables the policyholder to fulfil their long-term financial goals. The sum assured in the plan depends on the premium amount, policy tenure and the premium paying term of the plan.
Why should a person purchase an Endowment Policy?
Buying an endowment policy can be a smart way to build savings and protect your family’s future. These policies are simple to understand and easy to follow. You pay a fixed premium regularly—monthly, quarterly, half yearly or yearly—and in return, you get two main benefits. First, if you live (in case you are the life assured ) till the end of the policy term, you receive a lump sum amount. This amount can be used for big life goals like your child’s education, wedding, or buying a home. Second, the nominee gets the death benefits if life assured dies during the policy tenure.
Many endowments plans also offer bonuses (if declared), which means the total payout at the end could be even higher. Some plans even offer the policyholder the option to add riders such as like accidental death benefit
or critical illness riders at an additional nominal premium.
You also enjoy tax benefits on the premium you pay and the payout you receive. These are available under Section 80C (only under the old tax regime) and 10(10D) of the Income Tax Act, if all conditions are met.
Types of Endowment Plans in India
Endowment life insurance plans encompass a variety of options, each tailored to meet the unique insurance requirements and diverse financial objectives of individuals. Endowment plans can be unit-linked or non-unit-linked insurance plans. Here are the different types of endowment life insurance plans available in the market3:
01
-->
Regular Endowment Plans
Regular endowment plans are the simplest form of savings plans
which provide insurance protection and the ability to create a financial corpus. Such plans pay a maturity benefit if the insured survives the policy tenure. However, if the insured passes away during the policy tenure, a death benefit is paid.
02
-->
Whole Life Endowment Plans
Whole-life endowment plans provide coverage for the entire lifetime of the policyholder i.e. till 99 or 100 years of age.
03
-->
Child-Oriented Endowment Plans
As its name suggests, these endowment plans are designed to safeguard your child’s financial future by providing a mix of life cover and savings. The policy benefit can be used for your child’s education, marriage, or other financial needs. These plans generally have an in-built premium waiver benefit, in which in the event of the death of the parent during the term, the plan does not stop. The premiums are waived off for the remaining premium payment tenure and the policy continues unaffected.
04
-->
Money Back Plans
Money-back endowment plans ensure periodic payouts as survival benefits during the term of the policy offering liquidity to meet specific financial goals and paying the balance amount on maturity if the insured survives the entire term.
05
-->
Participating Endowment Plans
Participating endowment plans entitle the policyholders to bonuses by allowing them to participate in the profits of the insurance companies. These accumulated bonuses enhance the death or maturity benefit payable.
06
-->
Non-Participating Endowment Plans
Unlike participating endowment plans, these plans do not entitle the policyholder to participate in the profits of the insurance company and do not earn bonuses.
What are the Benefits of Opting for an Endowment Plan?
Endowment plans are beneficial for individuals who are looking for a savings instrument along with life protection. It helps you cultivate a habit of savings. Some of the key benefits of an endowment plan are stated as follows:
Peace of mind
Endowment plans act as a safety net for your family and provide you peace of mind that your family is financially secure even when you are not around.
Corpus creation
With endowment plans, you can create a stable corpus for your financial goals. You can choose a tenure that aligns with your goals and get insurance coverage plus savings creation so that your goals can be fulfilled.
Bonus enhances the corpus
If you choose participating endowment plans, you might earn bonus additions during the policy tenure. These bonus additions enhance the corpus of the policy and help you earn a higher policy payout.
Financial planning
Endowment plans to aid in effective financial planning. These plans are structured to provide long-term savings, enabling you to achieve specific goals such as buying a house or car, child education or marriage, etc.
Tax benefits
The premiums paid for endowment plans are eligible for tax deductions up to Rs. 1.5 lacs under the 80C of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (the Act) subject to specific conditions. The premium of up to 10% of the capital sum assured qualifies for tax deduction for policies issued on or after 1 st April 2012. For policies issued before this date, premiums up to 20% of the capital sum assured are allowed as a deduction. If you are suffering from a disease or disability specified under Section 80U or 80DDB, premiums paid up to 15% of the capital sum assured will be allowed as a deduction for policies issued on or after 1 st April 2013.This deduction is available only under old regime of Tax.
The death benefit paid is completely tax-free. The maturity benefit paid is also exempted from tax under Section 10 (10D) of the Act if all conditions under section 10(10D) are satisfied.. For policies issued on or after 1 st April 2023, if the annual aggregate premium paid is more than Rs.5 lakhs during the term of such policy/policies, the gain from such policy would be taxable in the hands of recipient
Salient Features of an Endowment Policy
Here are some of the salient features of an endowment policy:
Endowment plans have flexible premium payment terms
and modes. Premiums can be paid monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or annually.
Certain plans can have in-built coverage such as accidental death benefits, income benefits, and premium waiver benefits without any added premium costs.
These plans can be customized to suit individual requirements through add-on riders for enhanced protection 5 .
Some endowment plans offer joint life cover providing coverage to both policyholders in a single policy.
Some endowment plans offer an option to receive the death benefit or maturity benefit in a lump sum, as monthly instalments or partly in lump sum and party as instalments.
These plans have a grace period of 15 days or 30 days from the due date, based on the premium payment mode within which the premiums are to be paid to keep it in force failing which it will lapse and the benefits will cease.
You can avail of a loan on your endowment plan
based on the specific terms and conditions stated in the policy document 4 .
Riders in Endowment Plans
Riders are a supplementary feature which enables you to customize the coverage of your insurance plans to suit your individual insurance needs and enhance the overall coverage of your base plan. Riders come at a nominal premium and can be bought at the time of buying or renewing the policy. Some common add-on riders in endowment plans
include the following:
01
-->
Accidental Death Benefit Rider
Accidental Death Benefit Rider provides a lump sum pay-out on accidental death of life insured during the term of the policy. The benefit under the rider is paid in addition to the sum assured in the base endowment policy
.
02
-->
Accidental Permanent Total and/or Partial Disability Benefit Rider
Accidental Permanent Total and/or Partial Disability Benefit Rider offers financial aid by providing a lump sum amount to the insured in the event of permanent disability due to an accident depending upon the rider's terms and conditions.
03
-->
Family Income Benefit Rider
Family Income Benefit Rider pays a regular income to the family of the life insured for a certain period on the first occurrence of death, first diagnosis of covered critical illness or accidental permanent total disability during the term of the policy.
04
-->
Critical Illness Benefit Rider
Critical Illness Benefit Rider provides coverage from the specified critical illnesses and pays a lumpsum amount upon diagnosis of covered critical illnesses such as heart attack, cancer, paralysis, kidney failure, etc. during the term of the policy 6 .
05
-->
Waiver of Premium Benefit Rider
Waiver of Premium Benefit Rider provides the benefit of waiver of all the future premiums of the base insurance policy and applicable riders, subject to the prescribed maximum limit, on the earlier occurrence of untimely demise, diagnosis of critical illness or accidental total and permanent disability suffered by the insured 7 .
What Should You Look For Before Purchasing An Endowment Policy?
Before you buy an endowment policy, check your financial goals, the plan’s features, and how it helps your future needs. Choose a policy that matches your savings and protection goals.
Goals of the policyholder
Before buying any plan, it is important to assess what you want to achieve. Do you want to save for your child’s education? Do you want money for your retirement? Or do you want to make sure your family is protected if something happens to you? Your goal will help you choose the right endowment plan with the right policy term and sum assured.
Endowment plan features
Every plan has its own set of features. Some offer fixed returns. Some offer bonuses. Some plans give money monthly; others give a lump sum.
Check how long the policy lasts, how often you need to pay premiums, and how much money your family will get if something happens to you. Also, check if the plan allows riders (like accident cover), flexible payouts etc.
Importance of an endowment plan
An endowment plan is important because it gives two benefits. First, it protects your family with life cover. Second, it helps you save money over time.
If you survive the policy term
, you get a lump sum, which you can use for big goals like buying a house or your child’s wedding. If you die during the policy, your family gets the sum assured. This way, you can secure your future and your family’s future too.
What Are The Limitations Of Endowment Life Insurance Plans In India?
Endowment plans offer guaranteed* returns, but they also come with limits like low returns, low liquidity, and long-term lock-in.
Lower Returns
Endowment plans are safer, but the growth of your money is may be slower.
Liquidity
If you need money urgently, you cannot easily withdraw from an endowment plan. You may need to wait for a few years or pay a penalty to get your money early.
Inflation
The payout amount is fixed in advance. But over time, prices go up due to inflation. So, the money you receive later might not have the same value in the future.
Cost
Premiums for endowment plans are usually higher than those for term insurance. You are paying for both savings and life cover, which can be expensive if your income is limited.
Reasons to Add Riders to Endowment Plans
Adding riders to endowment plans increases the safety rating of the basic insurance plan and benefits through customization and maximizing the benefits of life insurance coverage. Here are some of the reasons to add riders to your endowment plans:
Extended coverage
It extends the coverage protecting specific events or risks such as accidental death, specified critical illness, etc.
Accelerated benefits
Some add-on riders offer a portion of the benefit in advance upon diagnosis of the specified illness assisting in effective medical care and addressing immediate financial concerns.
Waiver of premium
Some riders such as waiver of premium benefit rider and accidental permanent total/partial disability benefit rider ensure that the policy remains in force even after the death of the policyholder or their inability to pay the premiums due to disability. The premiums are waived and paid by the insurance provider 7 .
Eligibility Criteria for Buying an Endowment Plan
The Eligibility criteria to buy an endowment plan vary for different endowment policies. Some of the common eligibility requirements are as follows:
Entry Age
Endowment plans have a minimum and maximum entry age criterion. Individuals falling within the minimum and maximum age bracket can buy the endowment policy.
Maturity Age
The maturity age is the maximum age up to which coverage is allowed. Some endowment plans offer coverage for the entire life of the insured. For example, under Whole Life Endowment Plans, the maximum maturity age is usually 99 years or 100 years.
Sum Assured
The eligibility criteria might also specify the minimum and maximum sum assured limits. You can choose a suitable coverage within this limit.
Premium term and premium payment term
Endowment plans have predetermined policy terms and premium payment terms. The policy term is the duration over which the coverage is provided. The premium payment term is the duration over which premiums are payable. The premium paying term can be regular (premiums payable throughout the chosen tenure), limited (premiums payable for a limited number of years) or single (premiums payable at once).
Who Can Buy an Endowment Policy?
Endowment plans are ideal for the individual looking for insurance solutions and long-term savings for their financial goals. Endowment plans are well-suited for the following 3 :
Young individuals who want to secure their future and fulfil their long-term financial goals by creating a substantial corpus over time
Individuals who are seeking peace of mind that their family is financially covered at all times.
Individuals who are looking for insurance coverage plus savings
Parents planning for their child’s higher education, marriage, etc.
Individuals with a low to moderate risk appetite as these plans ensure guaranteed * returns. 10

Endowment Plan Claim Process
The claim process of endowment plans involves several steps and the beneficiaries and policyholders need to follow them to ensure a smooth claim settlement. These steps may vary from insurer to insurer. The general steps for claim process are as follows:
Claim Reporting
The first step in the claim process is reporting the claim to the insurance company. follow these simple steps to report your claim:
Fill and print the form
Go to the official website of the insurance company and fill out the form with policy details such as policy number, your name, PIN code, email ID, phone number, etc. or visit the nearest branch for completing the formalities.
Attach the documents
Attach relevant documents depending on the type of claim such as death claim, critical illness claim, and disability or hospitalization claim.
Submit at the nearest branch
Submit the form and relevant documents at the nearest branch of the insurance provider. You can locate the nearest branch on the official website of the insurance provider.
Claim Processing
After you submit the form and relevant documents, your claim will be processed. The insurance company will assess the documents submitted for processing of the claim and will reach out to you, if any additional documents are required.
Claim Settlement
Once all the documentation is completed, the insurance company endeavours to settle or reject the claim within 30 days from the date of receipt of the last necessary document. As mandated by the Insurance Regulation and Development Authority of India (IRDAI), if a claim requires investigation, it shall complete the investigation for the claim within 90 days of the receipt of claim intimation and the claim shall be settled within 30 days thereafter. In case of rejection of the claim, the insurance company will communicate the reason for such rejection to the beneficiaries/policyholder.

Frequently Asked Questions
1. Do all endowment plans in India offer life coverage?
Endowment plans, typically provide both a savings component and life cover, often extending to whole life cover up to 99 years or 100 years of age. A portion of the premium for endowment plans goes towards savings and the remaining goes towards providing life cover.
2. How is an endowment plan considered to be different from a term life plan?
While both plans offer life insurance protection, the major difference between the two is the savings component. Endowment plans offer a savings element and pay a benefit on maturity whereas term plans usually provide life risk cover by paying death benefits. Term plans don’t usually offer maturity benefits but if you choose the return of premium term plan, your premiums are refunded on maturity
3. What is the concept of additional bonuses in an endowment plan?
Generally, there are two types of additional bonuses associated with endowment plans which include reversionary bonuses, which are annually declared by the insurance company and terminal bonuses which are usually paid on surrender or maturity, subject to the terms of the policy and financial performance of the insurance provider.
4. Does an endowment plan offer additional bonuses along with the guaranteed benefit when the endowment policy matures?
Endowment plans might offer terminal bonuses as additional bonuses based on the terms of the policy with the maturity benefit when the endowment policy matures.
5. How does the premium get determined for an endowment plan?
Premiums for an endowment plan are determined based on several factors such as the insured’s age, no. of individuals covered, sum assured, medical history, policy term, number of riders added, etc. You can use the online premium calculator to determine the premiums of the endowment plans.
6. When is the correct time to buy an Endowment Policy?
There is no right time to buy an endowment policy. Buying an endowment policy depends on the financial goals of each individual. It is advisable to buy one as early as possible to get low and affordable premiums, enjoy insurance protection at a younger age and build a corpus over time.
7. What is the policy tenure of an Endowment Plan?
The tenure of an endowment policy depends on the specific plan. The tenure of the policy is selected by the policyholder at the time of buying the plan. Some endowment plans have a limited tenure and may start from as low as 5 years and might extend to whole life cover up to age 100 years.
8. Who is eligible to invest in an endowment plan?
Individuals meeting the specific eligibility requirements, generally within a specific age bracket can invest in endowment plans. It is necessary to check the terms and eligibility of the specific endowment policy to know if you are eligible to invest in an endowment plan.
9. Can I opt for an endowment plan for the benefit of my child?
Many insurance companies offer endowment plans specially designed for children. In these plans, the child is the beneficiary while the parent is the policyholder. In the event of the unfortunate demise of the parent/policyholder, some child endowment plans
have an in-built premium waiver benefit in which the future premiums are waived off and the policy continues undisturbed. Child endowment plans are tailored to meet your child’s specific financial goals such as their higher education, marriage, etc.
10. How many times can the nominee be changed in an Endowment Plan?
You can change the nominee in your endowment policy as many times as you want by simply endorsing the policy to the new nominee.
11. Does the endowment plan pay death benefits?
Endowment plans do provide a payout on death if the insured passes away before the end of the policy term.
12. What happens if the premium is discontinued in Endowment Policies?
If you discontinue the premiums of your endowment policy, your policy will lapse and the coverage benefits of the policy will cease. Some endowment plans offer a grace period during which you can pay the outstanding premiums to keep it in force. If you don’t pay the premium within the grace period
, your policy might acquire a paid-up value.
13. What documents are needed to buy an Endowment Policy?
Commonly required documents to buy an endowment policy include age proof, address proof, ID proof and photographs along with a duly filled application form. Additional documents may be needed based on the insurer’s requirements.
14. How does taxation work in Endowment Plans?
Generally, premiums paid and benefits received under the endowment policy are eligible for tax exemptions up to specified limits under Section 80C and Section 10 (10D) of the Income Tax Act of 1961, respectively. However, the exemption is available subject to the satisfaction of certain conditions specified therein as mentioned above. If policy is not satisfying conditions as mentioned under Section 10(10D) of the Income Tax Act, gain from such policy will be taxable at applicable slab rate in the hands of recipient.
15. Is an Endowment Plan considered to be good?
Endowment plans are savings-oriented life insurance plans that guarantee * benefits. Its suitability depends on the financial goals of each individual. It can be a suitable choice for individuals looking for a combination of life cover and savings. However, it's necessary to consider the specific needs of each individual and compare them with other investment options.
16. What happens if a policyholder discontinues to pay the premium for the Endowment Plan?
In case the policyholder stops paying Endowment Life Insurance Policy Premiums, the policy may lapse, and the coverage benefits may be affected. Depending on the terms of the policy, the lapsed policy might run at a reduced paid-up value, which is paid on premature demise or maturity.
17. Is the endowment plan safe?
Yes, endowment plans are considered comparatively safer because they offer fixed returns and life cover. They do not depend on market volatility , so your money is not at risk due to market ups and downs. These plans are good for people who want guaranteed* savings and peace of mind. However, you should also check if the plan fits your goals and budget before buying.
