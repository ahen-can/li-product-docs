What is Life Insurance
Life insurance is a financial protection plan that provides a payout (sum assured) to the nominee in case of your unfortunate death during the policy term. It helps your family manage daily expenses, repay debts, and maintain financial stability in your absence.
When you buy a life insurance policy, you pay a regular premium to the insurer. In return, the insurance company guarantees a death benefit payout to your nominee. Depending on the plan, some policies may also offer savings or investment benefits .
Life insurance is essential for individuals with financial dependents , as it secures important goals like children’s education, home loans, and retirement planning .
In simple terms, life insurance acts as a financial safety net , ensuring your loved ones remain financially secure even when you are not around.
Life Insurance Riders You Should Consider
Critical Illness Rider : Provides a lump sum payout if you are diagnosed with specified critical illnesses like cancer, heart attack, or stroke. Helps cover medical expenses and loss of income.
Accidental Death Benefit Rider : Offers an additional payout over the base sum assured if death occurs due to an accident, providing extra financial security to your family.
Waiver of Premium Rider : Waives off all future premiums if you suffer from disability or critical illness , ensuring your policy continues without financial burden.
Accidental Disability Rider: Provides a regular income or lump sum if you become partially or permanently disabled due to an accident.
Income Benefit Rider: Ensures your family receives a monthly income instead of or along with a lump sum payout, helping manage regular expenses.
Terminal Illness Rider: Pays a portion of the sum assured if you are diagnosed with a terminal illness , helping manage treatment and other costs.
Hospital Cash Rider: Provides a daily cash allowance during hospitalization, helping cover incidental expenses not included in medical insurance.

Aviva India offers various types of life insurance policies to suit different goals, life stages, and risk profiles. Whether you’re looking for pure protection or a mix of insurance and savings, there’s a plan designed to meet your specific needs. Here’s a closer look at the most common types of life insurance cover:
Different Types of Life Insurance Policies in India
1. Term Insurance
This is the simplest and most affordable form of life insurance. It offers high coverage for a relatively low premium. The policy provides a death benefit to the nominee if the policyholder passes away during the term. However, there is no payout if the policyholder survives the policy period. It’s ideal for those who want maximum financial protection for their family at minimal cost.
2. Whole Life Insurance
Unlike term insurance
, whole-life policies provide lifelong coverage—typically up to 99 or 100 years. The death benefit is guaranteed; some policies may also build cash value over time. This accumulated value can be borrowed against or used for future needs. Whole-life plans suit those looking to leave a legacy or ensure long-term financial security for dependents.
3. Endowment Plans
Endowment plans
combine insurance with a savings component. If the policyholder survives the term, a lump sum is paid as the maturity benefit. If they don’t, the nominee receives the death benefit. These plans often come with guaranteed returns and bonuses, making them appealing for individuals looking to achieve specific financial goals while staying insured.
4. ULIPs (Unit Linked Insurance Plans)
ULIPs offer a dual advantage—life cover along with market-linked investment. A part of the premium goes toward insurance, and the rest is invested in equity, debt, or balanced funds. ULIPs
provide transparency and flexibility in fund choices, making them ideal for goal-based investing. However, returns depend on market performance, so they carry investment risks.
5. Money Back Policies
These are structured to provide liquidity during the policy term. A fixed percentage of the sum assured is paid out at regular intervals. The remaining amount, along with bonuses (if any), is paid at maturity. Money-back plans
are good for individuals who want periodic payouts to cover planned expenses.
6. Child Plans
These are designed to secure a child’s future, particularly their education and career goals. The plan offers a lump sum at maturity, and in the event of the policyholder’s demise, future premiums are waived, but the policy continues. It ensures your child’s dreams are not disrupted, even in your absence.
7. Retirement Plans
Also known as pension plans
, these help you build a corpus for your post-retirement years. During the accumulation phase, you pay premiums to build savings. Upon maturity, you can convert the corpus into an annuity that provides regular income after retirement. These plans offer financial independence in your later years.
When choosing a life insurance policy, assessing your financial responsibilities, dependents’ needs, and long-term goals is important. Each policy type serves a distinct purpose—so finding the right fit can make all the difference in your overall financial plan.
Benefits of Choosing the Right Life Insurance Plan
Selecting the right life insurance plan isn’t just about protection—it’s about aligning your financial decisions with your long-term goals. The right policy can offer peace of mind today and financial resilience for tomorrow. Here are some key benefits of life insurance
:
Financial Security
Life insurance ensures your loved ones are financially protected even in your absence.
It acts as a safety net, covering ongoing expenses like EMIs, school fees, or household costs.
Some plans offer add-ons like accidental death or disability cover for added security.
Maturity Benefits
Plans like endowment and ULIPs offer a lump sum payout if the policyholder survives the term.
These benefits can help fund life goals such as home buying, children’s education, or retirement.
Some policies also include loyalty additions or bonuses, enhancing the final maturity amount.
Death Benefit
The core feature of any life insurance policy is the death benefit paid to the nominee.
This amount helps maintain your family’s lifestyle, settle liabilities, and manage future needs.
It provides immediate financial support during a difficult time, reducing monetary stress.
Tax Benefit
Life insurance premiums qualify for deductions under Section 80C
of the Income Tax Act.
Maturity and death benefits are also tax-exempt under Section 10(10D), subject to conditions.
These exemptions make life insurance a smart choice for tax planning.
How to Select the Best Life Insurance Policy for Your Needs
Choosing the right life insurance policy
can feel overwhelming, especially with many available options. However, selecting the best plan becomes easier when you align it with your financial goals, responsibilities, and life stage.
Start by assessing why you need life insurance. Are you seeking pure protection, planning for a child’s education, retirement savings, or combining insurance with investment? Your purpose will help narrow down the type of policy—term plans for affordability, endowment or money-back plans for guaranteed returns, and ULIPs for market-linked growth.
Next, consider your current life stage and income. A young professional with few responsibilities may opt for a high-cover term plan, while someone with dependents may require protection and savings. Parents planning for their child’s future or individuals nearing retirement may choose goal-specific plans like child or pension policies.
Another key factor is coverage amount. A common rule is to have a sum assured at least 10–15 times your annual income, but you should also factor in existing liabilities, ongoing expenses, and future goals.
Policy tenure should align with your financial obligations. For instance, if your children will be financially independent in 20 years, your policy term should ideally cover that duration.
Also, look into additional features like life insurance riders
( critical illness
, accidental death), the insurer's claim settlement ratio, and flexibility in premium payment options.
Finally, compare plans from different insurers—not just on price but also on benefits, service reputation, and long-term value. A well-chosen life insurance policy isn’t just a safeguard—it’s a step toward financial peace of mind.
Why Should Women Consider Investing in a Life Insurance Policy?
Life insurance is often viewed as a necessity for primary earners, but it's equally important for women, regardless of whether they’re working professionals, entrepreneurs, homemakers, or caregivers. Investing in a life insurance policy can offer women financial independence, protection, and long-term stability.
A life insurance plan safeguards working women's income contribution to the household. It ensures that their loved ones won’t face financial hardship in the event of an untimely demise. For women with loans or other liabilities, life insurance
helps prevent that burden from falling on family members.
While not salaried, homemakers contribute significantly through unpaid work. Their absence can result in added childcare and household expenses. A life insurance policy helps account for that value, offering financial support during a difficult transition.
Life insurance plans with savings or investment benefits can also help women achieve personal goals—such as funding a child’s education, starting a business, or planning retirement.
Additionally, life insurance provides tax benefits and can serve as a low-risk investment avenue for long-term planning.
Ultimately, life insurance empowers women to take charge of their financial future, making it a key component of any well-rounded financial strategy.

Tax Benefits for Life Insurance Plans
Deduction under Section 80C : Premiums paid for life insurance plans are eligible for tax deduction up to ₹1.5 lakh per year under Section 80C, helping reduce your taxable income.
Tax-Free Maturity Benefits (Section 10(10D)): The maturity or death benefit received is generally tax-free, subject to conditions specified under Section 10(10D).
Tax-Free Death Benefit: In case of the policyholder’s demise, the sum assured paid to the nominee is fully tax-exempt, ensuring financial security for your family.
Additional Benefits for Riders: Premiums paid for riders like critical illness or disability may qualify for deductions under Section 80D, as per applicable rules.
Long-term Tax Efficiency: Life insurance plans offer a tax-efficient way to save and protect wealth, making them a key part of financial planning.

Life Insurance Plans Related FAQs
How does a life insurance policy work?
When you buy a life insurance policy, you pay regular premiums to the insurer. In return, the insurer promises to pay a sum assured
to your nominee in case of your death during the policy term. Some plans also offer maturity benefits if you outlive the term.
Which type of life insurance plan is the most affordable?
Term insurance is the most affordable type of life insurance. It offers high coverage at low premiums but only pays out if the policyholder passes away during the policy term.
Can life insurance premiums be tax deductible?
Yes, premiums paid toward life insurance policies are eligible for tax deductions under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, up to ₹1.5 lakh per financial year.
Can I withdraw money from my insurance policy?
Withdrawals are not allowed in term plans. However, in ULIPs and traditional plans like endowment or money-back, partial withdrawals or loans against the policy may be permitted after a certain period.
What are the tax benefits of buying life insurance plans?
In addition to deductions under Section 80C, the payout received—either as a death benefit or maturity benefit
—is usually tax-free under Section 10(10D), subject to policy conditions.
