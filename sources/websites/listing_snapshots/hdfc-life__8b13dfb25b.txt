What is a Savings Plan?
Saving plans are life insurance plans
designed to help you save for the future. They encourage regular saving habits and can offer substantial returns when needed. The best savings plans also provide insurance coverage, ensuring your family’s financial security in case of unforeseen events.
These plans typically offer a fixed maturity benefit when the policy term ends. Some plans also provide regular income during the policy period or as part of the maturity proceeds. Selecting the right plan with flexible features can help you achieve your life goals, protect your family in unexpected situations, and build a fund to cover future expenses.
Savings plans can be combined with additional benefits, such as death benefits and critical illness riders
. These plans are a great way to start saving with low-risk options that also include insurance coverage. For more options, explore different saving schemes
that fit your needs.

Why invest in a savings plan?
A savings plan plays a vital role in achieving your financial goals and ensuring long-term security. It helps you stay prepared for life's uncertainties, whether it's handling emergencies, purchasing a home, or funding education. Additionally, while planning for long-term security and identifying the safest place to put your retirement money
, having a structured investment plan plays a crucial role in safeguarding your family's future. By starting early and choosing a plan that aligns with your risk appetite, you can steadily build your financial resilience and create a strong financial foundation. Understanding the importance of saving
enables you to make informed financial decisions and stay committed to your long-term goals. Prioritizing savings today sets the stage for a stable and prosperous future for you and your loved ones. Take the first step now and invest in your future security!

A Money Back Plans
is a life insurance product that combines the benefits of investments and insurance. If the policyholder survives the set policy term, a guaranteed sum called the maturity benefit plus bonuses is paid out by the insurer.

How to Choose the Best Saving Plan?
Choosing a savings plan is a crucial step toward securing your financial future. It helps you achieve both short-term goals, like buying a car, and long-term ones, like retirement. Before you start, assess your current financial situation, including your income, existing debts, and family's needs. To find the best plan, consider factors like scope of life insurance coverage, plan features, growth potential, flexibility, and tax benefits. Also, make sure to check the insurer’s claim settlement ratio for long-term reliability.
01
Assess Plan Features and Benefits
Compare core features like the payout structure (e.g., lump sum vs. regular monthly income), premium payment term, and maturity benefits. Aligning these features with your short- and long-term goals ensures the plan remains relevant. Look for plans with the flexibility to change premium terms or benefit payout options if your needs evolve.
02
Use a Savings and Income Calculator
Using a savings and income calculator helps you estimate the ideal premium amount and maturity value needed to reach your financial goals. This tool allows you to compare different scenarios and adjust inputs based on your budget. For the most accurate results, use calculators on reputable insurer websites, as they are tailored to their specific plans.
03
Check for Emergency Withdrawal Options
Liquidity is key, as unexpected emergencies can arise before a plan matures. Look for plans that allow partial withdrawals after a specific lock-in period. Be sure to check for any penalties or fees associated with early withdrawals to avoid unexpected losses and ensure you understand the terms fully.
04
Explore Rider Options for Enhanced Coverage
Riders** are optional, additional benefits that can be added to a policy, such as critical illness, accidental death, or waiver of premium. They provide a cost-effective way to boost your protection without buying a separate policy. Choose riders that align with your lifestyle risks and health conditions to get the most value.
05
Evaluate Risk Tolerance and Return Potential
Match the plan's return profile—whether it's guaranteed or market-linked—with your personal risk tolerance. Guaranteed plans offer stability and predictable returns, while market-linked options carry higher risk for the potential of higher returns. For a balanced approach, consider a mix of both guaranteed and growth-oriented components.
06
Consider Tax Benefits
Many savings plans offer dual tax benefits: deductions on premiums paid under Section 80C# of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (corresponding Income-tax Act 1961) , and tax-free maturity proceeds under Section 10(10D)# of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (corresponding Income-tax Act 1961) . These benefits significantly enhance the overall value of the plan. Always verify that the tax benefits are current and applicable under the latest tax regulations to avoid future surprises.

Factors Impacting Savings Plan Premium
A number of crucial factors can impact the premium you pay on the plan you choose.
Age
This is the primary factor that decides the premium you pay, although some savings plans are age agnostic as well. At a younger age, a person is less likely to suffer from an age-related disease and pass away prematurely, hence the premiums are lower. You can lock in a good savings plan at lower premium rates when you opt for a savings plan at a younger age. The insurer increases the premium rates as your age goes up, as the chances of a claim (due to death) also increase.
Policy period and sum assured
There's usually a higher premium if you opt for a longer policy period, since the probability of claims is higher.Choosing a term that terminates with your retirement and a policy amount that is enough to protect your family's financial needs is the wise option.
Medical history
A history of diseases that have tendency to run in the family and your own medical records as well as parameters like height and weight can raise flags about potential illness or obesity, and can impact the amount of premium you pay.
Smoking and drinking habits
Since research indicates that people who smoke are likely to contract more illnesses than those who do not and also have a higher mortality rate, insurance premiums for smokers tend to be higher than for non-smokers. The same goes for excessive drinking habits. It is prudent to disclose your smoking and drinking habits to the insurer before purchasing a plan.
Occupation
Some professions or lines of work carry more risk and hence, insurers might charge a higher premium. It is important to make proper disclosures about your work and income to the insurer before finalising the policy.
Riders
Additional riders like critical illness or accidental death cover can attract an additional premium since you are opting for extra benefits by opting for such riders.

Common Mistakes to Avoid in a Savings Plan
Savings plans offer a valuable tool for building a secure financial future for yourself and your family. However, strategic investment decisions are crucial to maximise returns and minimise risks. Here, we explore some common pitfalls to avoid when investing in a savings plan:
Over-Exposure to Single Market Instrument
Many savings plans offer investment in a variety of market instruments like equity, debt, or gold. While focusing on a single asset class might seem appealing, it exposes your portfolio to undue risk.

Over-Investing
Over-enthusiasm can lead to neglecting essential financial buffers. While contributing to a savings plan is commendable, prioritise building an emergency fund first. Experts recommend having 3-6 months' worth of living expenses readily available to handle unexpected situations.
This financial safety net allows you to continue investing in your savings plan with peace of mind, knowing you can address emergencies without derailing your long-term goals.

What Happens if I Stop​​ Paying Premiums for my Savings Plan?

Sometimes, if premiums are paid for a minimum number of years, the paid-up value allows the policy coverage to continue with reduced benefits.

How do Savings Plans Compare with ULIPs (Unit Linked Insurance Plans)?
Savings plans and ULIPs both combine insurance with investment, but differ significantly in structure and risk. Savings plans offer guaranteed or defined maturity amounts, making them suitable for conservative investors seeking stable, low-risk returns.
ULIPs, on the other hand, are market-linked, offering flexible fund options and higher growth potential, though with increased risk. While ULIPs allow for fund switching to achieve better liquidity, savings plans are generally more rigid in their approach.
Understanding the savings plan vs ULIP comparison helps investors weigh guaranteed vs market-linked returns and the key difference between ULIP and savings plan.

Can I take a Loan Against my Savings Plan?
Yes, you can take a loan against a savings plan; most traditional savings plans, such as Public Provident Fund (PPF) and life insurance policies, allow loans.
To avail of a policy loan, the policyholder typically needs to have a surrender or paid-up value of at least 2 to 3 years’ premiums. The loan amount from a savings account normally consists of 80% to 90% of the surrender value.
The primary benefit of a loan against savings plan is its financial liquidity. Policyholders can avail loans without even surrendering their policy. However, if the loan repayment is not complete, it drastically reduces the maturity or death benefits.

How does Inflation Affect the Value of my Savings Plan's Maturity Amount?
Inflation's impact on the savings plan's maturity amount can be drastic since inflation erodes the purchasing power of fixed maturity benefits. For example, a ₹10 lakh payout today from a savings account may not suffice 15 years later. That way, insufficient fixed returns from traditional savings plans in the future could make your financial planning futile.
Therefore, incorporating inflation-adjusted savings in your financial planning is crucial. To achieve this, combine market-linked products for inflation protection with traditional savings plans.

22
Can I increase my premium or investment amount during the policy term?
Yes, some savings plans enable policyholders to increase their contributions using plan upgrades or top-ups. For example, in a ULIP (Unit Linked Insurance Plan), there is a top-up option that allows policyholders to increase their contributions. Increasing premiums on your savings plan will enable you to build a larger corpus while ensuring long-term returns.
23
Are savings plans suitable for short-term financial goals?
No, saving plans are primarily suitable for mid and long-term financial goals because of their compounding benefits. If you are looking for something that could help in reaching short-term goals, choose plans with high liquidity or low risk, such as fixed deposits and recurring deposits.
24
Can I switch between funds in a market-linked savings plan?
Yes, switching between funds in a market-linked savings plan is a great option to manage your investments effectively. The Unit Linked Insurance plans or ULIPs enable policyholders to switch funds across equity, debt and hybrid funds based on their market performance.
Please note that switching funds helps manage risk, but does not make the investments completely risk-free. So, whenever a particular fund is performing better than the other, it yields more returns. That way, investors can take advantage of market movements easily.
25
What is the difference between guaranteed and non-guaranteed savings plans?
Whereas guaranteed savings plans protect your investments from market volatility while offering predetermined returns, non-guaranteed savings plans offer variable returns that depend entirely on market performance. Compared to a guaranteed savings plan, a non-guaranteed savings plan is riskier and has the potential for higher returns.
26
Do savings plans provide life cover along with savings benefits?
Yes, savings plans offered by HDFC Life combine both life insurance protection and wealth creation. Plans such as HDFC Life Sanchay Plus(UIN: 101N134V26), or HDFC Life Sanchay Par Advantage(UIN: 101N136V04), are some of the most popular savings plans that provide guaranteed benefits and life cover. These plans ensure wealth creation while offering financial protection to the policyholder’s families.
27
How can I track the performance of my savings plan?
To track the performance of your savings plan, you can utilise online tools such as the HDFC Life Savings calculator
. Besides these online portals, you can check annual statements or fund value updates. Finance experts recommend tracking your savings annually to have a clear understanding of your financial health.
28
Can I change the nominee in my savings plan later?
Yes, you can change the nominee in your savings plan at any time simply by submitting a service request online or offline. It is mandatory to keep your nominee details updated, as this will help you gain faster claim settlement.
29
What are the surrender charges if I exit my savings plan early?
Exiting a savings plan early, especially within a lock-in period, could lead to reduced benefits and surrender charges. These charges depend on the type of policy you own and the time remaining until maturity. To avoid such unnecessary charges, it is ideal to stay invested as long as possible to receive maximum maturity value.
30
Are bonuses or loyalty additions applicable to all savings plans?
No, loyalty additions and bonuses do not apply to all savings plans. Traditional savings plans such as endowment plans offer reversionary bonuses, guaranteed additions or loyalty additions. These types of benefits mainly depend on the policy type, performance and policy term.
31
How does compounding work in long-term savings plans?
When a policyholder earns returns from both the initial investment amount and the accumulated interest amount, it is called compounding. It is a snowball effect that helps in growing your money over time. Therefore, experts recommend investing early so that the investment amount has sufficient time to grow through the magic of exponential compounding.
