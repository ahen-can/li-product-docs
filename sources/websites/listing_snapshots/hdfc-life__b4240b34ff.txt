Retirement and Pension Plans
A retirement pension plan helps you save consistently during your working years and gives you a regular income after you retire.
These plans provides you with either a regular monthly pension or a lump sum amount at maturity in return for the investment you make during your earning years. The income can help you manage essential expenses and maintain your lifestyle after retirement.

What are Retirement and Pension Plans?
Retirement plan is a form of life insurance plan
that is designed to provide income and financial security after retirement.
Retirement plan refers to any strategic financial arrangement made by an individual to gather funds for their post-employment years. The primary objective of a retirement plan is to help an individual achieve financial freedom after retirement.
Pension plan is a specific type of retirement plan (offered by employers through government schemes or as individual pension plans) that provides a predefined regular income (known as pension) post-retirement in exchange for regular investments in a pension fund. The predefined regular income or payout is provided through the purchase of annuity plans
once the retirement corpus has been accumulated in a pension plan.

How Do Pension Plans Work?
Pension plans in India are designed to help you build a corpus while you are working to ensure that you get a steady stream of income after your retirement. Pension plans have two phases:
Accumulation phase :
During the accumulation phase, you need to regularly invest in a pension plan. Your investments will compound over time and grow to build your corpus.
Vesting phase :
Once you reach your predefined retirement age in India
(for example, 60 years), also known as the vesting age, you will start receiving a regular pension or annuity from your accumulated corpus.
Let’s understand how different types of pension plans work in India:
Government-Backed Pension Schemes
These are popular for their steady returns, making them suitable for individuals seeking a low-risk retirement plan.
a. Employees’ Provident Fund (EPF)
How it works: This is a compulsory pension plan for salaried individuals in companies that have 20 or more employees. As per the Employees’ Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952
vii both the employee and the employer chip in 12% of the employee’s basic salary and their Dearness Allowance (DA) into the EPF account. 8.33% of the employee’s pensionable salary (with a limit of Rs.15, 000), is directed towards the Employees’ Pension Scheme (EPS), while the rest goes into the EPF.
Benefits: When someone retires or leaves their job, the total corpus (along with the accrued interest) in EPF can be availed. The EPS provides a monthly pension after retirement if the individual has at least 10 years of service and retires at 58. They can get a reduced pension starting at 50. Plus, it offers family benefits, such as a pension for widows or children, if the EPF account holder passes away .
Tax Benefits: Contributions towards EPF are eligible for tax deductions under Section 123 of the Income Tax Act, 2025 (corresponding to Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, 1961) up to the overall ceiling limit of ₹1.5 lakh in a tax year vii .
b. National Pension System (NPS)
How it works : National Pension System
(NPS) is a voluntary retirement savings scheme for all Indian citizens aged between 18 and 70. Contributions made in the pension account are invested in a mix of equities, debts and government securities. Subscribers of NPS can choose their investment strategy based on their preferred asset allocation or can opt for the ‘auto choice’ option, which adjusts the investment
mix as you age.
Flexibility: NPS offers the freedom to choose pension fund managers and scheme option.
Payout: In case of normal exit
when the subscribers turn 60, they can withdraw up to 60% of the accumulated corpus as a lump sum. The remaining 40% of the corpus must be used to purchase an annuity plan from an Annuity Service Provider (ASP) to receive a regular pension viii .
Tax Benefits: Contributions are eligible for tax deductions under Section 123 of the Income Tax Act, 2025 (corresponding to Section 80C & Section 80CCD(1) of the Income Tax Act, 1961), whereby an individual contributing towards NPS can claim deduction up to 10% of salary (basic pay + DA) in case of employee and upto 20% of Gross Income for self-employed within the ceiling of Rs.1.5 lakh in a tax year. Further, an additional deduction of upto Rs.50,000 under Section 124 of the Income Tax Act, 2025 (corresponding to Section 80CCD(1B) of the Income Tax Act, 1961) for self-contribution to NPS over and above the aforesaid limit of ₹1,50,000.
c. Atal Pension Yojana (APY):
How it works : Atal Pension Yojana
is a government-backed pension scheme introduced for the unorganized sector. APY is applicable for all Indian citizens aged 18 to 40. The subscribers can exit the scheme at 60 and get a monthly pension.
Benefits: Under Atal Pension Yojana the subscriber will get a guaranteed minimum monthly pension between Rs.1000 to Rs. 5000 X
d. Public Provident Fund (PPF):
How it works: PPF can be treated as a pension plan to secure your retirement. PPF is a long-term savings scheme for all Indian citizens with a minimum deposit of Rs.500 per year
and a maximum deposit of Rs.1,50,000 per year. A PPF account matures on completion of 15 years xi .
Benefits: PPF offers guaranteed, tax-free returns and is considered a very safe and reliable investment option. The accumulated amount in a PPF can be used for retirement needs. Loan facility can be availed from the 3 rd financial year up to the 6th financial year . The withdrawal facility is permissible every year from the 7th financial year.
Tax benefits: Deposits made in a PPF are eligible for tax deductions under Section 123 of the Income Tax Act, 2025 upto ₹1.5 lakh in a financial year, and the interest earned and maturity amount are exempted from tax subject to prescribed conditions under Section 11 of the Income Tax Act, 2025 (corresponding Section 10(11) & Section 10(12) of the Income Tax Act, 1961).. xi
Private Pension Plans (Annuity Plans)
Annuity plans
are offered by life insurance companies in India and allows you to convert a lump sum or regular payments into a guaranteed income stream till you live or for a specified period.
How they work :
In an annuity plan, you pay a fixed premium either as a lump sum or through regular contributions to your chosen life insurance provider. In return, the insurer offers you a guaranteed income (annuity) for a specified period or for the rest of your life. This income can start immediately ( Immediate Annuity
) or after a deferment period ( deferred annuity
). To estimate your potential payouts and choose the right option, you can use an annuity calculator
based on your investment amount and retirement goals.
Types of annuity plans :
You can choose the type of annuity
depending on your needs:
Immediate Annuity Plans: As the name suggests, you pay a lump sum and start receiving a monthly pension immediately
Deferred Annuity Plans: You make payments over a set period (accumulation phase), and then you receive the monthly pension after a chosen deferment period. HDFC Life Smart Pension Plus
is a single plan offering both immediate & deferred annuity.
Life Annuity: In this case the pension amount is paid to you for lifetime. You can choose it to be a ‘single life’ (only for you) or ‘joint life’ (for you and your spouse).
Life Annuity with Return of Premium: With this option you will get your pension for life but on your demise your nominee will get back a certain percentage of the paid premiums. With HDFC Life Smart Pension Plus you have the option to avail life annuity with return of percentage of total premiums paid.
Annuity Certain: The pension amount is paid for a guaranteed number of years, even if you pass away. If you die before the end of the guaranteed period, your nominee will receive the remaining pension amount.
Guaranteed Period Annuity: This is similar to annuity certain, but with a specific duration like 5, 10, 15 or 20 years.
Pension Plans with Life Insurance (ULIPs): This pension plans is Unit Linked Insurance Plan
(ULIP) that combines the benefits of life insurance and investments. A portion of the premium goes towards providing life cover and the rest is invested in market-linked assets/funds. Returns are strictly dependent on market performance. HDFC Life Smart Pension Plan
is a suitable plan, if you want a regular income in your retirement by building the corpus with market linked returns in the accumulation phase. The plan comes with the advantage of life insurance.
Tax Implications: Tax deductions on premiums for private pension plans can be claimed under the Section 123 of the Income Tax Act, 2025 upto overall ceiling limit of ₹1.5 lakh in a tax year.
Choosing the right pension plan depends on your financial goals, risk tolerance, current age & retirement age and employment status. Understanding the difference between pension and provident fund
can also help you compare retirement savings options before choosing the most suitable plan. You can use tools like the retirement calculator and consult a financial advisor to determine the most suitable pension plan.

What are the tax benefits of pension plans in India?
Tax benefits on pension plans in India vary depending on the specific product and relevant tax laws. Generally, contributions to pension plans may qualify for deductions under applicable income tax provisions of Section 123 of the Income Tax Act, (earlier Sections 80C, 80CCC and 80CCD(1) of the Income Tax Act, 1961), which together allow up to ₹1.5 lakh in deductions in a tax year.
If you invest in the National Pension System (NPS), you may also claim an additional ₹50,000 under relevant provisions of Sections 124 of the Income Tax Act, 2025(corresponding to Section 80CCD(1B) of the Income Tax Act, 1961), making total deduction available up to ₹2 lakh in each financial year.
These benefits are available to eligible taxpayers and may vary depending on the type of plan. The tax treatment for withdrawals or pensions depends on prevailing rules such as the exemption for commuted pension under relevant provisions of Section 19(1) of the Income Tax Act, 2025 (corresponding Section 10(10A)) of the Income Tax Act, 1961) and may change over time.

Retirement plans in India have various features that help individuals secure their financial needs post-retirement. Features depend on the type of retirement plan such as government backed schemes, annuity plans and life insurance based options. Here are the common features in retirement plans:
Long-Term Savings
The Primary objective of retirement plans is to build a handsome corpus over the long-term (till the time you retire) in a sustained manner.
Regular Income Post-Retirement
Retirement plans provide a steady and reliable source of income after retirement, either in the form of a monthly pension (annuity) or a lump sum amount.

Tax exemptions are a significant advantage offered by most retirement plans in India. Contributions made, interest earned and withdrawal amounts are often eligible for tax deductions under various sections of the Income Tax Act, 2025.

Nomination Facility
In retirement plans you have the option to nominate your beneficiary. In case of your death your nominee will receive the accumulated fund or the monthly pension amount.
Flexibility
Although retirement plans are long-term investments
and you should invest in a disciplined manner, many plans offer flexibility in terms of vesting age, investment frequency (monthly, quarterly, annually or lump sum), investment choices/asset allocations (for market-linked plans), and withdrawal options in case of emergency. Life Insurance
based retirement plans offer the added benefit of life cover along with the opportunity to invest for retirement needs. HDFC Life Smart Pension Plan provides a life insurance cover to the extent of 105% of the total premiums paid, including top-up premiums.

How to Calculate the Return on a Pension Scheme?
Calculating the return on a pension scheme
helps you understand how effectively your investment is growing over time. It considers your contributions, accrued interest rates, and the overall performance of the selected plan. You should also evaluate the income tax on pension
at the time of withdrawal, as taxation can impact your net retirement corpus.
Here are some other elements of calculating the return on a pension scheme:
Understanding the Types of Returns
Pension schemes provide three broad categories of returns, which are given below:
Types of Returns in Retirement Plans
Description
Guaranteed Returns
Give fixed income irrespective of the market’s performance. These are ideal for risk-averse investors seeking stability and predictable payouts.
Market-linked Returns
Fluctuate with the market movement. They offer higher growth potential but come with investment risk based on market trends.
Bonus or Additional Returns
Usually provided by insurers upon performance or surplus. These returns act as extra earnings and can enhance the overall value of your pension corpus.
These categories help investors select a scheme that suits their risk tolerance and ultimate retirement aspiration.
Using a Retirement Calculator
A pension calculator is a basic online tool that calculates your retirement corpus based on crucial inputs like monthly contribution, rate of return, and investment term. You provide these inputs, and the calculator estimates the maturity amount, helping you plan accordingly.
It is a fast, convenient way to see future returns and make a well-informed decision for a financially sound retirement.
The Impact of Contribution Amount and Duration
Increasing your monthly contribution or extending your investment duration can significantly increase your retirement savings.
For instance, investing ₹2,000 monthly ## for 20 years at 8% returns yields around ₹30 lakhs ## , but extending to 30 years grows it to over ₹75 lakhs ## . This compound effect shows how consistent, long-term investments dramatically enhance your pension corpus over time.
## Premium may vary based on various factors
Considering the Rate of Return
The return on investment has a significant impact on your pension corpus since higher returns cause higher growth due to compound interest. For instance, investing ₹10,000 per month for 20 years at 6% will give you around ₹46 lakhs, whereas at 10% it reaches almost ₹76 lakhs.
Market-linked plans involve approximation of reasonable returns based on historical patterns and risk considerations. Therefore, recognising this correlation helps in formulating realistic goals and selecting an optimal investment strategy for retirement planning.
Accounting for Inflation
Inflation decreases your retirement corpus value in the future, so it is crucial to target returns that exceed inflation. For example, if your scheme has an annual return of 7% but inflation is 5% per annum; your actual return is merely 2%.
In 25 years, this can significantly decrease your corpus value. Hence, choosing pension schemes that provide returns higher than inflation ensures your retirement income retains its worth and meets your future lifestyle demands.
Tax Implications on Pension Returns
Deduction under Section 80C –
Investment in notified Pension scheme of UTI or mutual funds provide tax relief under Section 123 of Income Tax Act,2025 during the accumulation stage up to ₹1.5 lakh in a tax year
Deduction under Section 80CCC –
Section 123 Contribution to pension fund of insurance company also provide the deduction maximum upto Rs 1,50,000 in a tax year.
Deduction under Section 123–

1
What is a retirement corpus? And how is it different from a pension?
A retirement corpus is the wealth you create over time in order to support your life after you stop your regular 9-5 job. A pension, in contrast, is the regular income you get from that accumulated corpus or an annuity plan.
While a corpus endows you with flexibility in how you manage your funds, a pension ensures a stable as well as predictable income throughout your retirement. In totality, the plans, when combined, provide financial security over the long term and great mental peace.
2
What are the essential components of a well-rounded retirement plan?
A well-rounded retirement plan is one that brings together all the elements required to safeguard your financial future as well as ensure a steady income in the years ahead. Important components are:
A retirement corpus supported by pension or annuity income
Holistic health insurance plan and sufficient life cover
A dedicated exigency fund for meeting unanticipated expenditures
Tax-efficient planning via financial options such as income tax deduction provisions (earlier covered under Section 80C
) and NPS
.
Inflation-adjusted planning to maintain buying power
Clear estate as well as nominee arrangements for smooth benefit transfer
Such components together create a perfectly balanced, secure, and future-ready retirement strategy/plan.
3
When is the best time to start planning and saving for retirement?
The best time to begin your retirement planning is as soon as possible. Beginning early endows your funds enough time to grow via the compounding effect, as well as minimises the pressure to save massive amounts later on.
Early starters create their retirement fund with greater ease. But late starters require allocating more from their month-on-month income. Planning ahead ensures financial comfort as well as security over the long-term period.

When should I start planning for retirement (based on life stage)?
You can begin with retirement planning at any phase. But the focus must change as you move through your life:
Twenty–thirty years before retirement: Concentrate on creating a sufficient corpus via consistent savings as well as long-term investments
.
Ten–fifteen years before retirement: Examine your assets, adjust your investment portfolio, and pragmatically strengthen your retirement strategy/plan.
Approaching retirement: Optimise taxes well; figure out income needs as well as withdrawals and annuity payouts.
Such a phased approach assists you in remaining prepared, financially secure, and confident regarding your future.

What is the importance of insurance in retirement planning?
Insurance plays a vital part in safeguarding your retirement savings. A health insurance policy assists in better managing unanticipated medical expenditures, preventing massive withdrawals from your retirement fund. A life insurance policy

Participating pension plans share a part of the insurer’s profits with policyholders in the form of bonuses/dividends. Non-participating plans, however, endow just guaranteed benefits without sharing profits.

Participating plans ~ might appeal to those looking for long-term value with occasional upside. You may be required to pay capital gains tax if you exceed that threshold, under applicable capital gains provisions of Section 198 of the Income Tax Act, 2025 (corresponding to Section 112A of the Income Tax Act, 1961) as LTCG at applicable rates of 12.5% on gains exceeding the prescribed limit of ₹1.25 lakh under the head "Income from Capital Gains".
Tax exemptions generally depend on factors such as:
The relationship between the premium paid
The sum assured
Whether the policy follows the rules defined for eligible ULIPs
If these conditions are not met, withdrawals may be treated as taxable income.
22
Can NRIs invest in Indian retirement or pension plans?
Yes, NRIs can invest in many Indian retirement and pension plans, subject to the specific product and the applicable regulations. Most plans require the following from NRIs:
Complete KYC formalities
Provide overseas address proof
Comply with the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 (FEMA) for permissible investments
Participation rules may vary across insurers and pension products. The tax treatment for NRIs can differ, based on their country of residence and local tax agreements. However, certain benefits available to residents may not be applicable. Therefore, it is advisable to review plan-specific conditions and the latest regulatory requirements before investing.
23
How much retirement corpus do I need in India?
The retirement corpus you need in India depends on your lifestyle, expected inflation, current age, and whether you have dependents. A common guideline is the income-replacement rule. It suggests planning for about 70–80% of your present income during retirement, adjusted for rising costs over time. This helps ensure you can maintain a similar standard of living.
Since everyone’s expenses and goals are different, it is helpful to use retirement calculators that factor in inflation, years to retirement, and expected spending. These tools give a personalised estimate and make it easier to plan how much you may need to save consistently.
24
How much should I save monthly for retirement?
It is generally suggested to aim to save at least 15% of your annual income each year for retirement. Starting to save for retirement early; helps because your savings get more time to grow. How much you should save each month for retirement depends on:
Your age
How many years do you have until retirement
The return assumptions you are comfortable with
As your income increases, gradually increasing your contribution can keep you on track. A goal-based approach works best. Estimate your future expenses, retirement age, and desired lifestyle. Then use a retirement calculator to understand how much you may need to save regularly. This keeps your plan realistic and aligned with your long-term goals.
25
Should I save for my retirement or my child’s education first?
It is generally wiser to prioritise retirement planning first, because education loans are available for children, but no loans to fund your retirement years. Saving early for retirement also gives your money more time to grow through compounding, which can ease the pressure later in life.
A goal-based approach helps in the following ways:
Plan separately for retirement and education
Review how much each goal may require
Allocate savings accordingly
This way, you stay prepared for your child’s future without compromising your long-term financial security.
26
Can I continue investing in my retirement plan after retirement?
You can continue investing in your retirement plan after retirement, but it depends on the type of product. In ULIP-based pension or retirement plans, you may keep contributing during the accumulation phase if the plan allows it. However, once you buy an annuity plan, further contributions are not permitted, as the product is designed to provide regular income.
Continuing your investments can help build more stability over time. You can also adjust the frequency of your contributions based on your financial comfort. Reviewing your plan features regularly ensures your post-retirement strategy stays aligned with your needs.
27
What happens if I stop paying premiums?
What happens when you stop paying premiums depends on the type of retirement plan you have. In many traditional or ULIP-based plans, the policy may become paid-up if you have paid the required minimum premiums. Still, the benefits usually decrease in proportion to the premiums paid.
If the policy is discontinued too early, surrender charges may apply, which can impact the overall corpus or pension you eventually receive. Before stopping premiums, it is helpful to check whether your plan offers premium holiday or alteration options, so you can stay protected without disrupting your long-term goals.
28
Can I switch funds in ULIP-based retirement plans?
Yes, ULIP-based retirement plans
usually allow you to switch between equity, debt, or balanced funds, depending on your investment goals. Switching helps you stay aligned with your risk appetite, respond to market movements, and gradually shift to more stable options as you approach retirement.
Most plans offer a certain number of free switches, with additional switches available according to plan-specific rules. It is best to switch funds thoughtfully, keeping your time horizon, comfort with risk, and long-term retirement needs in mind so your strategy stays balanced and consistent.
29
What is inflation-adjusted retirement planning?
Inflation-adjusted retirement planning
means to estimate your future expenses after accounting for rising prices. Over 15–25 years of retirement, the cost of healthcare, daily living, and other essentials can increase significantly, so planning with today’s expenses alone may leave a gap later.
By factoring in inflation, you can work towards a corpus that keeps up with future costs. Some people also explore annuity options that offer increasing payouts, subject to product features, to help manage rising expenses. Using retirement calculators can help you model future values and understand how inflation may impact long-term planning.
30
Should I choose a monthly or yearly pension income?
Choosing between monthly and yearly pension income depends on your spending habits, cash flow needs, and your comfort level with managing money. A monthly pension is well-suited if you prefer a steady income for day-to-day expenses and like the reassurance of regular inflows.
An annual pension may suit you if you prefer a lump sum for bigger planned expenses and feel confident managing a larger amount at once. The tax treatment is usually similar, but it is best to check the details for your specific plan. Ultimately, choose the option that aligns with your lifestyle, comfort, and financial discipline.
