# Bharti Life: findings, FY21 to FY27 year to date

Prepared by S3 on 5 Nov 2026 for deliverable 01. Every number below is a row in `bharti_trend_data.csv`, and the tables are in `bharti_analysis.xlsx`. Page numbers are PDF pages.

Source names used below:
- PD FY22, PD FY23, PD FY24, PD FY25: `fy22-pd.pdf`, `fy23-pd.pdf`, `FY24-pd.pdf`, `fy25-pd.pdf`.
- PD FY26: `fy2025 - L1-to-L45-consolidated-q4.pdf`. The file name says FY25, but it is the FY26 year-end PD.
- PD Q1 FY27: `q1-fy27-pd.pdf`.
- AR FY21 to AR FY26: Bharti annual reports. FY23, FY25 and FY26 are scans, read by OCR and checked by the user.
- Product Performance: Bharti's KPI disclosure files. FY24 to FY26 tables cover only the 8 products on today's 90% list.

## Your two hypotheses

**"ULIP grew from about 10% to 17-19% of the book." This holds.** Linked premium (life, pension and group) was 11.3% of gross premium in FY21, fell to 9.7% in FY23, and reached 17.3% in FY26 (PD FY22 p4, PD FY23 p3, PD FY26 p2, L-1-A-RA). In new business, linked was 22.6% of individual NBP in FY21, 12.5% in FY23 and 33.0% in FY26 (L-22 row 3; PD FY22 p29, PD FY23 p30, PD FY26 p29).

**"Non-par halved." This does not hold.** Non-par individual life rose from 44.6% of gross premium in FY21 to 58.0% in FY26; in rupees it grew from Rs 1,017 cr to Rs 1,890 cr (L-1-A-RA, same pages). The segment that halved is par: 38.2% of gross premium in FY21, 18.6% in FY26.

S1's reading in PROJECT_MEMORY section 5 is confirmed: linked share of individual NBP was 22.6%, 16.3%, 12.5%, **17.9% (FY24, now filled)**, 25.0% and 33.0%. S1's "non-par is 58% of total" matches non-par individual life; non-par including group (the deliverable 01 basis) is 62.1%.

## Observations

1. **Growth is slow and below peers.** Gross premium grew from Rs 2,281 cr in FY21 to Rs 3,256 cr in FY26, 7.4% a year (PD FY22 p4, PD FY26 p2). FY24 was flat (-0.4%) and FY25 grew 2.5%. FY26 growth of 9.3% was lower than 11 of the 13 peers; only ICICI (8.5%) and IndusInd Nippon (6.0%) grew less (deliverable 01 Data tab, from each insurer's L-1-A-RA).

2. **The mix moved from par to ULIP, with non-par savings still the core.** Par premium fell every year, from Rs 872 cr (FY21) to Rs 606 cr (FY26). Linked more than doubled, from Rs 258 cr to Rs 563 cr, with +25% in FY25 and +48% in FY26 (L-1-A-RA). In Q1 FY27, linked was 41.7% of individual NBP (PD Q1 FY27 L-22 p28).

3. **New business recovered only in FY26.** NBP (first year plus single) was Rs 783 cr in FY21, Rs 1,016 cr in FY23, then Rs 781 cr in FY24 and Rs 742 cr in FY25, before Rs 1,069 cr in FY26 (L-4; PD FY22 p8, PD FY23 p9, PD FY24 p9, PD FY25 p8, PD FY26 p8). FY26 first-year premium alone grew 48% (Rs 612 cr to Rs 905 cr).

4. **Bharti sells fewer, larger policies.** Individual new policies fell 41%, from 116,831 (FY21) to 69,123 (FY26) (L-38; PD FY22 p58, PD FY26 p58). Average ticket size for individual regular premium more than doubled, from Rs 51,350 to Rs 1,20,865 (L-22 row 25; PD FY22 p30, PD FY26 p30).

5. **Agency has collapsed and Direct has taken its place.** Individual agents brought 44.8% of individual NBP in FY21 and 13.8% in FY26; Direct went from 17.1% to 44.5%, and to 52.9% in Q1 FY27 (L-38; PD FY22 p58, PD FY26 p58, PD Q1 FY27 p54). Almost all Direct is "Direct, others", not the company website (online was negative in FY25 and FY26). Banks (10.1%), other corporate agents (17.8%) and brokers (13.9%) made up the remaining 42% in FY26.

6. **Persistency is weak and fell after FY24.** 13th-month persistency (premium basis, regular pay) rose from 62.5% (FY21) to 73.0% (FY24), then fell to 71.0% (FY25) and 64.5% (FY26). 61st-month persistency was 37.9% in FY26 (L-22 row 19; PD FY22 p30, PD FY24 p31, PD FY26 p30). Among the 14 insurers in the peer view, Bharti's FY26 13th month is second lowest, above only Shriram (60.9%); SBI is 87.6% and HDFC 84.9% (each insurer's FY26 L-22).

7. **Conservation is falling in par and linked.** Par life conservation fell from 84.7% (FY21) to 77.9% (FY26) and 73.2% in Q1 FY27; linked life fell from 84.4% to 74.4% (L-22 row 5; PD FY22 p29, PD FY26 p29, PD Q1 FY27 p28). Peers' FY26 linked life conservation is mostly 77% to 88%.

8. **Expenses of management were over the IRDAI limit every year from FY21 to FY25.** The excess charged to shareholders was Rs 200 cr (FY21), Rs 231 cr (FY22), Rs 210 cr (FY23), Rs 100 cr (FY24) and Rs 9 cr (FY25) (AR FY21 p130, AR FY22 p79, AR FY23 p120, AR FY24 p106, AR FY25 p86). FY25 EoM was 101% of the allowable amount (AR FY25 p87). FY26 is within the limit (AR FY26 p100).

9. **IRDAI's stance has changed.** IRDAI granted forbearance for every year from FY17 to FY24 (AR FY22 p79, AR FY23 p120, AR FY25 p87). For FY25 it issued a show-cause notice on 15 Jan 2026 instead; Bharti has replied and no decision is published (AR FY26 p41, p101; IRDAI Warnings and Penalties page, checked 5 Nov 2026). In Aug 2026 IRDAI warned Edelweiss Life and Pramerica Life for the same breach and barred them from opening new branches for six months (IRDAI press release, 21 Aug 2026). Under the 2024 EoM Regulations, forbearance is normally only for insurers in their first five years (Regulation 16, p18); Bharti's 3-year plan to comply by FY26 matches the one-time route in Regulation 17.

10. **The cost problem is operating expense, not commission.** Bharti's commission ratio was 8% of gross premium in FY26, below the private-life average of 9% in IRDAI's consultation paper (L-22 row 7, PD FY26 p29; Distribution Reforms Part 1 p46). EoM to gross premium was 34.2%, so other operating expenses were about 26% (approximate; L-22 rows 6 and 7). IRDAI gives an FY26 range of 6.1% to 27.7% for life insurers (Part 1 p44), which puts Bharti near the top.

11. **IRDAI's proposed EoM limits would be a large step for Bharti.** The consultation paper proposes life EoM of 15% within two years and 12.5% within five, with FY2027-28 as Year 1, and graduated penalties for insurers that miss the glide path, including limits on new product launches (Part 1 p43 Box 2, p44 para 97). Bharti's ratio on the closest published basis is 34.2%. The paper also proposes lower commission caps for banks, brokers and corporate agents than for agents, and 1% to 2% caps on single premium savings (Part 1 p47, p51). In FY26, 42% of Bharti's individual NBP came through banks, brokers and corporate agents, and 10.9% of individual NBP was single premium.

12. **Sales depend on a few products, with a launch cycle every year.** In H1 FY27, eight products made up 97% of NBP (90% list, Sep 2026). Four of them had no sales before FY25 or FY26: Growth Shield Plus (first sales FY25), Dream Shield Plus, Guaranteed Bachat Plan and Insta Gain Plan (first sales FY26) (Product Performance, FY24-FY26 tables). Guaranteed Wealth Pro, the largest product, fell from Rs 165 cr NBP in FY24 to Rs 113 cr in FY26.

13. **Annuity is growing from a small base.** Non-par individual pension (which includes Swabhimaan Retirement Plan) rose from Rs 16 cr gross premium in FY23 to Rs 63 cr in FY26 (L-1-A-RA). Swabhimaan NBP grew from Rs 6 cr (FY24) to Rs 38 cr (FY26) and Rs 34 cr in H1 FY27 alone; it was 12% of FY27 NBP to Sep 2026 (Product Performance; 90% list).

14. **Balance sheet and claims are not the weak points.** Solvency was 191% in FY26 against 178% in FY21 (L-22 row 21; PD FY22 p30, PD FY26 p30). AUM (L-12 + L-13 + L-14) roughly doubled, from Rs 9,374 cr to Rs 18,287 cr (L-3; PD FY22 p7, PD FY26 p7). The individual death claim settlement ratio by count has been about 99% every year (99.09% FY22, 99.21% FY26; L-40, PD FY22 p62, PD FY26 p62; same figures stated in the annual reports).

15. **Group business is volatile.** Group NBP went from Rs 136 cr (FY21) to Rs 215 cr (FY23), Rs 90 cr (FY25), Rs 131 cr (FY26) and Rs 73 cr in Q1 FY27 alone, when it was 38% of total NBP (L-37; PD FY23 p58, PD FY25 p57, PD FY26 p57, PD Q1 FY27 p53). In Q1 FY27, brokers brought 53% of group NBP.

## What the data cannot show

- **NBP by segment for FY25 and FY26 is derived, not disclosed.** No PD or annual report publishes it. We applied L-22 segment NBP growth to FY24 values; the derived segments add to within 18 lakh of the disclosed total. FY23 is reconstructed from a misprinted comparative in AR FY24 p124, confirmed by the user.
- **VNB, VNB margin and embedded value are not disclosed.** The FY26 PD lists L-44 Embedded Value as "Not applicable"; none of the annual reports FY21-FY26 mentions them.
- **Product-level data before FY24 is not available**, and the FY24-FY26 tables omit products that have since left the 90% list (those products were 46% of individual NBP in FY26).
- **Annuity is not separable from pension in L-1-A-RA.** L-36 splits annuity NBP but was not extracted.
- **The KPI spreadsheet starts at FY24.**

## Questions this raises

1. Has IRDAI decided the FY25 show-cause notice, and did Bharti receive an order like the Edelweiss and Pramerica orders of Aug 2026?
2. What is "Direct, others", now 53% of individual NBP? Is it employee sales, telesales or partner sales booked as direct? It replaced agency almost one for one.
3. Why did 13th-month persistency drop from 73.0% to 64.5% in two years? Is it linked to the shift to Direct or to newly launched products?
4. How does Bharti plan to cut EoM from about 34% to the proposed 15% by FY2028-29? Which costs make up the 26% non-commission expense?
5. What happened to the products that left the 90% list between FY24 and FY26, and what share of FY26 sales did they have?
6. Did Bharti or the Life Insurance Council ask for a longer glide path or relief for smaller insurers in the consultation (comments closed 25 Oct 2026)?
7. What does the 15% stake moved from Bharti Life Ventures to 360 ONE funds in FY26 (AR FY26 p90) mean for ownership and capital plans?
