Bharti product suite review, ULIP pilot, first draft

Bharti product suite: the ULIP pilot

Following our conversation on 3 November, this is my first attempt at the Bharti product suite analysis. I ran one category, ULIPs, end to end as a pilot. This page is not the analysis itself. It shows how we are working, so you can tell me whether the approach makes sense before I repeat it for protection, non-par savings, par and annuity.

  • A few products carry the sales. 8 products are 54% of FY26 individual new business. Four of them launched in FY25 or FY26, and the largest, Guaranteed Wealth Pro, fell from ₹165 cr to ₹113 cr in two years.
  • ULIP is a third of new business, up from 13% in FY23, led by Wealth Pro (₹82 cr) and Growth Shield Plus (₹80 cr, up 48% in its second year). No protection product is in the disclosed list.
  • Our hypotheses. ULIP grew from about 10% to 17% of the book: this holds. Par halved, from 38% to 19% of premium: this holds too.
  • Fewer, larger policies, sold direct. New policies fell 41% since FY21 while ticket size doubled. Agency fell from 45% to 14% of new business and Direct rose to 45%.
  • Weak persistency. 13th-month persistency is 64.5%, second lowest of the 14 insurers we compared.
  • Expenses over the IRDAI limit. Bharti breached the limit every year from FY21 to FY25, and IRDAI issued a show-cause notice for FY25. Expenses are 34% of premium, mostly operating costs rather than commission, against a proposed 15% limit.

06

Policy documents and feature list v5

For the 84 ULIPs, Claude read the policy document, customer information sheet and brochure, and recorded 65 fields per product, each with document, page and a quote. Each feature is recorded as one of:

built-inoptionalridernot offerednot stated

This found what brochures miss: 31 features not in v1 (such as a refund of 118% of all charges, or plans that continue after death with premiums funded), full charge and mortality tables, and 353 corrections to v1.

We then read the 30 riders these ULIPs name, because features such as waiver of premium, critical illness and accident cover often come only through a rider. Reading them changed the picture: waiver of premium went from 43 to 60 of the 84 products, and Bharti, which looked as if it had none, offers it on 6 of its 7 ULIPs through its Complete Shield rider. A separate session checked the list twice (about 1,700 checks); v5 has every correction and a few coding rules, such as splitting additions into a % of premium (amount known at the start) and a % of fund value (moves with the market).

What v5 shows about Bharti, early read:

Bharti has, most peers don'tWealth Maximizer refunds 118% of all charges. Only 3 of 84 ULIPs refund fund management charges, and one is Bharti's. 3 Bharti products return charges at a multiple. 6 of the 7 allow unlimited free switches; Future Invest gives 12 a year.
Peers have, Bharti doesn'tWaiver of premium built in or as an option (28 of 84; 60 have it in some form; Bharti has it only through its Complete Shield rider, at extra cost and not on death), top-ups (38; six Bharti documents are silent, one says not allowed), additions as a fixed % of premium (32; Bharti's are a % of fund, except Grow Wealth Ultra's for women and auto-pay), a plan that continues after death (16), and pension or child ULIPs. 07

Returns calculator

Insurers each define "net yield" differently, so we built one calculator that applies each product's published charges to the same customer, month by month:

Premium in. Allocation charge taken, units bought
Monthly charges. Admin, and mortality from the policy's own rate table
Fund grows at 4% or 8%; fund management charge taken
Year end. Loyalty additions, boosters and refunds added
Result. Fund at maturity and net yield

Standard customer: male 30, ₹1 lakh a year for 10 years, 20-year term, cover 10 times the premium, equity fund.

Checked by hand. I entered this customer into the insurers' own premium calculators and benefit illustrations for 5 products: SBI eWealth Plus, HDFC Click 2 Wealth and three Bharti ULIPs. All five match our calculator within 0.3%.
08

Shortlist: 84 to 14 to 5

Insurers don't publish sales by product, so we matched Bharti's best sellers instead of each peer's.

  • Bharti's anchors: Wealth Pro and Growth Shield Plus, its two largest ULIPs, and Wealth Maximizer for its unusual refund design.
  • One product per peer, the closest design: a life ULIP, regular or limited pay, sold offline, returning mortality charges where the insurer has such a plan.
  • Benchmarks: two online ULIPs and LIC Index Plus.

That gives 14 products in the comparison Excel. The deck puts Wealth Pro against SBI, HDFC, Tata AIA and Bajaj, the four largest private sellers with documents.

Questions for you

Pick an option for each, add a note if you like, then copy your answers and send them to me.

1. Is this the right approach overall?
2. How we choose the insurers to compare

The rule has three parts. Leaders: rank insurers by category premium and take them until they add up to 80%. For ULIP that is SBI 27.9%, ICICI 12.7%, HDFC 12.5%, LIC 11.8%, Tata 7.2%, Bajaj 6.2%, then Axis Max 5.8%, which takes us to 84%. Challengers: among smaller insurers, score each out of 1 on feature richness (40%), how specialised it is in the category (30%), growth (20%) and an expert nomination (10%, not applied yet, so the scores below are out of the first three). ABSLI scored 0.88, Canara HSBC 0.77 and Ageas Federal 0.66. Bharti is always in, and LIC is a reference column.

3. How we choose the peer products

Today: the closest design to Bharti's best sellers, because peers' product sales are not public.

4. The calculator's customer

Today: male 30, ₹1 lakh a year for 10 years, 20-year term, cover 10 times the premium. Other profiles are quick to add now the calculator exists.

5. The measure of size

Category sizing uses total premium, including renewals; Bharti's product view uses new business premium (NBP). APE is not disclosed by product or segment.
