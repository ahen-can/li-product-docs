ULIP Field Guide

A walkthrough for someone new to Indian life insurance. It starts with the five product types, then covers how a unit-linked plan works, every charge and feature in our feature list, who sells what, and where Bharti Life sits. Every number comes from this project's data: FY26 public disclosures, 516 brochures and 21 policy documents read field by field.

Chapter 1

Life insurance in India, in five product types

Every individual life product is registered with the regulator, IRDAI, and gets a UIN (unique identification number). For example, 130L100V03 is insurer 130 (Bharti), L for linked (N would mean non-linked), product 100, version 3. Every brochure must print a one-line description such as "A Unit Linked, Non-Participating, Individual Life Insurance Plan". We classify products from that line, never from the product name.

"Par" and "non-par" are about profits, not guarantees. A par policyholder shares in the insurer's profits through bonuses. A non-par policyholder gets only what the contract states. A ULIP is always "non-participating", because its returns come from the fund, not from bonuses.
Chapter 2

How a ULIP works

A ULIP is a mutual-fund-like investment and a term policy in one contract. The insurer runs the funds, deducts charges for the cover and its expenses, and pays the fund value at the end.

Premium inThe customer pays, e.g. ₹1,00,000 a year for 10 years.
Allocation charge outA % of each premium is kept for sales costs. The rest buys units.
Units in fundsThe customer picks funds (equity, debt, balanced), or an automatic strategy.
Monthly deductionsAn admin charge and a mortality charge (the cost of the life cover) are taken by cancelling units.
Daily FMCA fund management charge is built into the unit price (NAV).
AdditionsLoyalty additions, boosters or refunds of charges are added as units, by the product's rules.
PayoutDeath: the higher of the cover and the fund. Maturity: the fund value.

Worked example: Bharti Wealth Pro, year 1

Male, age 30, ₹1,00,000 a year, 10 years paying, 20-year policy, cover of 10 times the premium (₹10 lakh), equity fund, 8% gross return. Charges carry 18% GST.

Premium paid1,00,000
Allocation charge, year 1: 9% of premium, plus GST−10,620
Invested in units89,380
Admin charge: 0.15% of premium a month (₹150), plus GST, for 12 months−2,124
Mortality charge: about ₹9 lakh sum at risk × 0.977 per 1,000 (age 30 rate), plus GSTabout −1,000
Growth at 8%, less FMC of 1.35% plus GSTabout +5,500
Fund value at the end of year 1 (calculator output)91,713

"Sum at risk" is the cover minus the fund value. It shrinks as the fund grows, so the mortality charge falls over time even though the rate per 1,000 rises with age. The age-30 rate of 0.977 per 1,000 is from the mortality table on page 14 of the Wealth Pro policy document.

ULIPs have a 5-year lock-in. If premiums stop in the first 5 years, the fund (less a capped discontinuance charge) moves to a "discontinued policy fund" that earns a guaranteed minimum of 4% and pays out after year 5.

Why charge design matters for selling. A high year-1 allocation charge pays the distributor. A product with nil allocation charge (often online-only) suits a direct buyer. Refunds of charges at maturity let a product show high headline charges and still deliver a good maturity value if the customer stays the full term. Seven of the 21 products refund some charges.

What the customer gets back

Insurers must show a benefit illustration at two assumed gross returns, 4% and 8% a year. The net yield is the return after all charges, i.e. the IRR of premiums against the maturity value. "ULIP % of own book"
How much of an insurer's own premium is ULIP. Industry average is about 20%. SBI Life is 54% ULIP; LIC is 4%. A high figure marks a ULIP specialist.
Leader / challenger / anchor
Our selection: leaders are the fewest insurers that together reach 80% of ULIP premium. Challengers are scored on features, specialisation and growth (ABSLI, Canara HSBC, and Ageas, your pick). Bharti is the anchor we compare everyone with.
Chapter 7

Which products are popular

Insurers don't publish sales by product. Public disclosures give premium by segment (linked, par, non-par, pension) for each insurer, but not by product. So "popular" can only be judged indirectly:

What we have

Each insurer's ULIP size (chapter 6), and how many ULIPs each one runs: Tata AIA 14, Bajaj 12, ABSLI 10, Axis Max 9, PNB MetLife 9, Pramerica 9, and Bharti, SBI and HDFC 7 each. Bharti also publishes a "Product Performance" file with sales by product, and that is the one official product-level source we know of.

What would help

Flagship products named in insurers' investor presentations and annual reports. Product sales data from your industry contacts. These are proxies, and we label them that way in the deck.

Of the 140 ULIPs, 63 are plain wealth ULIPs, 50 return mortality charges (TULIP-type), 18 are pension ULIPs and 9 are child plans with a payor benefit (v1 sub-segments).

Chapter 9

Glossary

Annualised premium (AP)
The premium for one year, whatever the payment mode.
PPT / PT
Premium payment term (years of paying) and policy term (years of cover).
Single / limited / regular pay
Pay once; pay for fewer years than the term; pay every year of the term.
Sum assured
The life cover. ULIPs set a minimum multiple of AP: 7x or 10x in the products we read (lower above age 50), and 1.25x for single pay.
Sum at risk
Sum assured minus fund value. The mortality charge is levied on this.
NAV
Net asset value: the price of one unit, after FMC.
SFIN
The regulator's code for each fund, e.g. ULIF02715/09/2025ENIFTY100F130.
Lock-in
5 years for ULIPs. No full withdrawal before that.
Discontinued policy fund
Where the money goes if premiums stop in the lock-in. Earns at least 4%, FMC at most 0.50%.
Rider
A separate add-on (accident, critical illness, waiver) with its own premium and UIN.
Benefit illustration (BI)
The insurer's projection for a specific customer at 4% and 8%. Required at sale.
CIS
Customer information sheet: a short standard summary given at purchase.
Bancassurance
Selling insurance through a bank's branches. The main ULIP channel for bank-promoted insurers.
TULIP
Trade term for a ULIP that returns mortality charges, so the cover costs nothing on survival.
Saral / POS
Standard regulator-designed products (Saral) and simplified products sold by point-of-sales persons (POS).
